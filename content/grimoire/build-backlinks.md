Backlinks are the hardest thing in SEO to get. That is why they work. They are other websites vouching for you. Most businesses know this. So they publish something good and wait for the links to arrive. They almost never do.

Links don't show up because you deserve them. They show up because you asked.

## What is a backlink, and why does it matter?

A backlink is a link from someone else's site to yours, and search engines treat it as a vote of trust. It is the most common trait of top-ranking pages. Backlinko's analysis of [11.8 million Google results](https://backlinko.com/search-engine-ranking) found the number one result had roughly three times as many referring domains as positions two through ten.

That is a correlation, not a law. But I ran an SEO agency for years, and I kept seeing it: the sites that ranked were the sites other sites talked about.

Hard to get is the moat. If links were easy, your competitors would have them too.

## Why does waiting for links fail?

Waiting fails because nobody is looking for you. Good content is the reason a site says yes. It is not the reason a site finds you. The businesses that rank run an actual outreach process while everyone else waits to be discovered.

Ask yourself one question: do you have a backlink process or a backlink wish? Almost everyone has the wish.

## Which link building methods actually work?

Four methods work, and each one gives the other site something it wants in exchange.

1. **Expert quotes through HARO.** HARO (Help a Reporter Out) emails reporter requests to sources three times a day. [Featured.com bought it from Cision in April 2025](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/featured-com-acquires-help-a-reporter-out-haro-from-cision-823809336.html) and relaunched it. Answer one or two a day. The links that land come from news sites Google already trusts.
2. **Guest posts in your own industry.** Offer a real article to a site your buyers read. You give them content. They give you a link from a relevant domain. Relevance is the point. One link from your vertical beats ten from nowhere.
3. **Comparison pieces and listicles.** Sites resist linking to an article that is only about you. So write about the whole field: the top options in your category, you included. Other sites will share a fair comparison. Nobody shares an ad.
4. **The skyscraper move.** Find a competitor's page that earns a lot of links. Build a better version. Then email everyone linking to the original and tell them a better resource exists. Most sites want to link to the best thing. Some will switch.

| Method | What you give | What you get | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| HARO quotes | A fast, expert answer | News-site links + your name in print | Daily, small |
| Guest posts | A finished article | A relevant industry link | Per post, medium |
| Comparison pieces | A fair look at the field | Links from sites that cite the list | One build, then outreach |
| Skyscraper | A better resource | Links switched from a competitor | High, one-time |

## Are press releases backlinks in disguise?

Press releases are backlinks in disguise, and Google can see through the costume. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list links with optimized anchor text in distributed press releases as link spam. Google has also said press release links should be treated like ads.

So use a press release for what it does well: getting a story in front of reporters. If a journalist writes about you, that earned article is the link. The wire copy is just the delivery truck.

Business listings work the same way. They are worth having when your name, address, and phone match everywhere. Treat them as accuracy work, not a ranking hack.

## Does link building still matter for AI search?

Yes, but the mention now matters as much as the link. Ahrefs studied 75,000 brands and found [brand web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at 0.664](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/). Backlinks scored 0.218.

That changes which links you chase. A link buried in a footer helps Google a little. A quote where a reporter writes your name, your claim, and a link helps Google and gives the AI layer a sentence to repeat. HARO quotes and comparison pieces do both. That is why they sit at the top of my list.

I've written about this shift in [The Most-Mentioned Wins](/grimoire/the-most-mentioned-wins). AI repeats what it sees most often. Every earned mention is one more vote. It's the same thing I measure as [Share of Brand Voice](/grimoire/share-of-brand-voice).

## Isn't link building just spam?

Buying junk links is spam. Earning links is how the web was built to work. The test is simple: did a real person at the other site choose to link to something worth linking to? Guest posts, expert quotes, and better resources pass. Paid link farms and keyword-stuffed anchors fail.

My rule of thumb is that quantity beats quality. I mean that directionally, not literally. You are not chasing garbage. You are refusing to wait for one perfect link while a steady stream of good, relevant ones sits there for anyone willing to do the outreach.

## Where should you start?

Start with HARO, because it is free and the effort is small. Sign up as a source. Answer one request a day that you are qualified for. Be fast, specific, and quotable. Run it for thirty days before you judge it.

Most of your competitors will never do this. The daily effort is the moat.

Pick one method. Put it on the calendar. Stop waiting to be discovered.
