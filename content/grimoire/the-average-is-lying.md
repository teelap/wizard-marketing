Everyone says AI killed cold email. Then somebody holds up an average and calls it a cause of death.

[Instantly’s 2026 cold email benchmark](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026) reports a 3.43% overall reply rate and 10.7% or higher for the top 10% tier. That’s data from its own platform, collected January 1 through December 18, 2025. It counts replies, including replies to follow-ups, against total emails sent. It’s not the percentage of prospects who said yes.

That number isn’t lying to you. The story people hang on it is. The benchmark shows that results vary. It doesn’t tell you whether AI wrote the emails, whether a human reviewed them, or what caused the gap.

## Did AI actually kill cold email? That average doesn’t say

Replies are still happening. Some senders get a lot more of them than others. That’s enough to make me question a blanket obituary. It’s not enough to tell you what your campaign will do.

Start by looking at your own sends. Is the list full of people who could plausibly need this? Does the note give them a reason to care now? Are you getting replies that move anything forward, or just counting every “no thanks” as a win? A dashboard can tell you something happened. You still have to figure out what.

## Let AI draft. Make a person earn the send.

Here’s the rule I’d use even if I never saw the benchmark: let AI help with the first draft, then make a person answer one question. Why this person, with this offer, right now?

If the answer is a first name and a compliment the machine could paste into another hundred emails, you’re not done. Check the facts. Pick the detail that makes the offer relevant. Give the reader one clear thing to respond to. If you can’t think of a reason they should care, don’t send it. Better copy won’t fix the wrong list.

## Augmentation versus replacement: which seat did you hire AI for?

AI does the heavy lifting and the gross part. It can organize research, give you five drafts, or cut a long note down to size. A person still owns the list, the offer, the facts, and the decision to hit send. That’s the line I’d draw. It’s a recommendation, not something the benchmark tested.

This is the same disease I wrote about in [Rent the Trick, Own the Truth](/grimoire/rent-the-trick-own-the-truth). Feed AI nothing and it gives you the average everyone else can publish. Give it your real numbers, your actual read on the buyer, and one reason the note belongs in their inbox. Then read it yourself.

## Where this leaves you

Before you turn up the volume, take one audience and one offer. Have a person review the batch. Track replies, but separate the ones that open a conversation from the ones that tell you to go away. Change one thing at a time so you can learn what mattered.

That’s the same discipline the [Eight Dominoes](/eight-dominoes) runs on: a person owns the message, in sequence, before the tool ever touches it. The average is a starting point. The judgment is yours.
