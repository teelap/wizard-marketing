Every ad spends the trust of the thing it runs on. The ads that survive say so out loud. This is an ad. You watch it, we get paid, you get your thing. The ones dressed up as the real answer win the quarter and poison the platform.

I've bought ads for clients on most surfaces that sell them, from Google and Meta to DSP inventory. The pattern repeats. The ad works because the place is trusted. Then the ad starts eating the trust.

## Why does every ad poison the thing it feeds on?

Ads exist because attention exists, and attention exists because something organic earned it. A search engine that finds the right page. A feed full of your friends. A chatbot that answers your question. Sell that attention and you start spending it.

Call it the parasite rule. The ad needs the host to be healthy. Every ad makes the host a little less healthy. Push too hard and the ad kills what fed it. Every channel runs this clock. The only question is how fast.

## Which ad models are honest?

Two models are honest, and both admit the trade.

| Model | The deal | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pay to skip | Pay more and the ads go away | Netflix's ad tier, YouTube Premium |
| Watch the toll | Sit through a marked ad, then get your thing | YouTube pre-roll, the ad break in a free mobile game |
| The disguise | The ad wears the organic result's clothes | Sponsored posts styled as editorial, ads dressed as answers |

The first two are honest. You might hate an unskippable ad, but you know exactly what it is. People resent a toll. They don't feel tricked by one. That's a big gap. You can resent a toll and still trust the road.

The third is the poison. It gets the best short-term numbers because it borrows credibility it never earned. Then the bill comes. The FTC has already called ads formatted to look like editorial content deceptive. The audience called it first.

## Why did the audience stop clicking?

Because they learned. AT&T ran what's usually called the first banner ad on HotWired in 1994, and it reportedly drew a click-through rate around 44%. Typical display banners today land well under 1%.

The ads didn't get a hundred times worse. People got a hundred times better at reading the ad slots like a map and walking around them. Banner blindness became ad blindness. On some platforms it became platform blindness, where people stop trusting the feed itself.

That changes what a marketer is buying. You aren't buying a click. You're buying a moment from someone who already knows what an ad looks like and already resents being sold to. Hide the ad and you spend trust you can't get back. Mark it and you keep the relationship. The honest ad isn't the nice move. It's the one that compounds.

## What does the ChatGPT ad fight tell us?

It shows how fast the audience catches the tell. In early December 2025, paying ChatGPT users posted screenshots of prompts to connect Peloton and Target showing up under unrelated chats. OpenAI said they weren't ads and had no financial component. It didn't matter. They felt like ads. OpenAI's chief research officer said the company "fell short," and it switched the suggestions off within days.

Look at the timeline. Sam Altman called ads plus AI "uniquely unsettling" at Harvard in 2024 and said ads were a last resort. Then the bill came due. In February 2026, OpenAI started testing ads on ChatGPT's Free and Go tiers in the US.

And to its credit, the version it shipped is the honest one on paper. OpenAI says the ads are labeled as sponsored, sit apart from the answer, and don't change what ChatGPT tells you. Paid plans stay ad-free. That's pay to skip plus a marked toll. Both honest lanes, at once.

So the question for AI isn't whether ads arrive. They arrived. The question is whether they stay off to the side. The first time a sponsored answer gets dressed up as the real answer, the trust that made ChatGPT worth advertising on starts to drain.

## What should a marketer do with this?

Pick the honest lane before your audience picks for you.

1. **Label everything.** Sponsored means sponsored. Make it obvious, not technically compliant.
2. **Never borrow trust you didn't earn.** If your ad only works because it looks like the content around it, it's spending someone else's credibility, and eventually yours.
3. **Earn the organic mention too.** Ads rent attention. Being named honestly in the real answer is the asset that lasts. That's the whole case behind [Share of Brand Voice](/grimoire/share-of-brand-voice) and why [the most-mentioned brand wins](/grimoire/the-most-mentioned-wins) in AI search.
4. **Treat trust as a budget line.** Every ad buy spends some. Ask what you're getting back for it.

Hidden ads do work. For a while. The audience always learns the tell, and when they do, they punish the whole surface, not just you.

No spell here. Tell people it's an ad. The ones who stay are the ones worth selling to.
