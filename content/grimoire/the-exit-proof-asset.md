Build the business and you build something you will eventually sell. Build an audience that trusts you and you build the one asset nobody can take.

I have two exits behind me. Each time, the buyer got the business. Nobody got the people who knew my name. I've said it before and I'll keep saying it: they can't take that audience and that awareness away from you.

## Why does a personal brand survive a sale when a company brand doesn't?

A company brand is property, so it transfers. A personal brand is a relationship, so it can't.

When you sell a business, the brand is part of what the buyer is paying for. The name, the reputation, the search rankings, and the reviews all move to their side of the table at closing. That's the deal working as designed.

The audience attached to you is different. It lives in a habit people have: reading what you write, watching what you post, trusting your read on things. No purchase agreement can reassign a habit. The buyer owns the entity. You still own the attention.

| | Company brand | Personal brand |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns it after a sale | The buyer | You |
| What it's built on | Name, logo, rankings, reviews | Trust in one person's point of view |
| What happens when you leave | Stays with the company | Follows you to the next thing |
| Speed to build trust | Slower; logos don't talk | Faster; people do business with people |

## Why do people trust a person faster than a logo?

People do business with people. A logo can't hold a point of view, admit a mistake, or answer a question on camera. A person can, and that's where trust comes from.

The data agrees. In the 2024 Edelman and LinkedIn B2B thought leadership report, 75% of decision-makers said thought leadership had prompted them to research a product or service they weren't previously considering. That is a person's thinking pulling a buyer into a market they weren't in yet. Goldman Sachs Research projected the creator economy could reach roughly $480 billion by 2027, because attention now attaches to individuals.

Marketers need to make a return to people doing business with people. It's where the trust already went.

## What order should you build in?

Build the audience first. Let it grow the business. Keep it through the exit.

That order is my own story. My agency grew on a TikTok audience I started from zero: 400 followers in the first 30 days, a video that broke out on day 34, and eventually 110K+. That audience turned into over 10 leads a week for the agency. I wrote up [the full TikTok timeline here](/grimoire/tiktok-growth-case-study).

The audience was the engine. It grew the company to the point that someone wanted to buy it. Then the sale closed and the engine was still mine.

Run it the other way and the math gets worse. Build only the entity and you create something valuable that you fully hand over. The next venture starts cold: no list, no followers, no one waiting to hear what you're building.

## Isn't a personal brand just vanity?

No. Vanity is performing for applause. A personal brand is showing up with a real point of view and telling the truth on a schedule.

The difference shows in who sticks around. A persona attracts spectators. A real opinion attracts people who agree with you, and you lose the ones who don't. That trade is the point. Be yourself, be honest about it, and accept you won't sell to everyone. The people you do reach will be loyal.

Two other objections come up every time I make this case:

- **"I don't want to be on camera."** Then don't be. Write, record audio, run a newsletter, take a stage. The principle is a person with a point of view, not a face on a phone.
- **"It's too slow."** It is slow. It's also the one channel you can't lose. Paid reach stops the day the card stops. An audience that trusts you keeps compounding between ventures.

## How do you keep a personal brand from tanking the sale price?

Keep the trust personal and the operations impersonal. Buyers discount a company where every client relationship runs through the founder's phone.

The fix lives inside the business: a team that can deliver without you, written processes, and a company brand customers recognize on its own. What you keep at closing is attention and trust, not the client contracts. Those belong to the buyer, and they should.

The two assets do different jobs. The company brand is the one you're building to sell. The personal brand is the one you're building to keep. If you want a way to measure the second one, start with [Share of Brand Voice](/grimoire/share-of-brand-voice): how often people search for you and mention you by name.

## What do you own if you leave tomorrow?

Answer that question honestly. If you sold or walked away from your business tomorrow, what would you still own the day after?

If the answer is nothing, you've built an asset that transfers 100% to somebody else. Start on the one that doesn't. Pick one surface you'll keep showing up on. Post a real opinion there every week. Collect the emails of the people who stick.

Slow to build. Impossible to take. That's the exit-proof asset. Start this week.
