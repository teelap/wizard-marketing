One marketing channel that works beats five that limp. Pick the one your buyer already uses. Give it everything for a fixed window. Get a clean yes or no. Then add the next.

I've watched this mistake from inside a seven-figure agency and from the client chair. A business runs SEO, paid ads, email, Instagram, and a podcast nobody listens to, all at half effort. None of them work. The owner concludes marketing doesn't work. Marketing was fine. The focus was split five ways.

## Why do five channels at half effort fail?

Five channels at half effort fail because attention is finite, and every channel has a threshold it has to cross before it pays. Below that threshold, a channel just runs. Above it, the channel works. Split your effort five ways and nothing crosses.

It looks like diversification. It's dilution. Each channel gets a fifth of what it needed, each one underdelivers, and you pay all five bills anyway.

A channel that works is an asset. A channel that half-works is a tax you keep paying every month.

## What does going all in on one channel actually look like?

Going all in means one channel gets your best hours, a fixed cadence, and no excuses for a set window. I did it on TikTok.

When I committed to TikTok, I posted twice a day. Hundreds of videos. The first 30 days got me 400 followers. Around day 34, one video popped and took me to 10,000. By May 2024 I was at 70,000. Today it's 110K+. That one channel generated over 10 leads a week for my agency and took us international. I broke the whole run down in [the TikTok growth case study](/grimoire/tiktok-growth-case-study).

That result didn't come from talent on day one. It came from volume and commitment on one surface. At half effort, those first 30 days would have looked like proof that TikTok doesn't work. I'd have quit at 400 and never seen day 34.

## How do you pick the one channel?

Pick the channel where your buyer already spends attention, weighted by what you can realistically do well. Both halves matter.

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Where does my buyer already look? | A channel your buyer ignores can't work at any effort level. |
| Can I execute this every week? | A channel you can't sustain dies in month two. |
| Can I measure it? | No number means no verdict at the end of the window. |
| Does it suit how I communicate? | Writers win on search and email. Talkers win on video. |

The best channel in theory loses to the good channel you can pour yourself into. If you hate the camera, don't start with short video because a trend piece said so.

Peter Thiel made a sharper version of this point in *Zero to One*: one distribution strategy usually dominates all the others, and failing to nail even one is what kills companies. Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares built the Bullseye framework in *Traction* around the same idea. Test cheaply, then focus hard on the single channel that's working.

## How long should you commit before you judge a channel?

Commit 110% for a window you choose before you start, then judge it against a number you also chose before you start. Half effort can't tell you whether a channel works. Full effort for a set period can.

1. **Set the window.** Months for organic channels like SEO and short video. Weeks can be enough for paid ads.
2. **Set the success number.** Leads, sales, booked calls. Pick one and write it down.
3. **Protect the cadence.** Same output, every week, for the whole window. No skipping.
4. **Read the result as a fact.** Yes or no. Not "it's kind of working."

A clean answer is something you can act on. Five blurry signals is a feeling, and feelings are how budgets disappear.

## Doesn't this contradict showing up everywhere?

No. Showing up everywhere is the destination. One good channel at a time is the road there.

The brands that feel omnipresent didn't launch on seven platforms in one week. They mastered one surface, learned what their message sounds like when it lands, and then carried that proven message to the next channel. Channel two starts from strength instead of from scratch. Channel three starts from more strength.

Launch five at once and you don't get "everywhere." You get five half-built storefronts and a tired team. The best channel also changes as you grow. The one that gets you your first 100 customers may not get you the next 1,000. So you run the process again, from a foundation that already works.

## What should you do this week?

Count the channels you're "doing" right now. Then count how many are working, meaning they produce leads or sales you can point to. If the honest answer is zero, you don't have a marketing failure. You have a focus failure.

Pick the one with the best shot. Pause or strip the rest down to maintenance. Set the window and the number. Then give that one channel everything you were spreading across five.

Focus protects your team's energy as much as your results. Nobody burns out running one channel well. Plenty of people burn out running five badly.

Sequence beats spread. It's the same principle behind [the Eight Dominoes](/eight-dominoes): get the first thing right, and the next one falls easier.

No spell here. Get one channel right. Then add the next.
