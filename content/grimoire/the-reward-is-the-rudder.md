TikTok did not kill short form. It stopped paying for the disposable version.

I built 110,000 followers on that platform posting twice a day for years, hundreds of videos, nearly all of them well under a minute. That account generated over 10 leads a week for my agency and took the business international. So when the panic started that short form is dying, I had a reason to go check the terms instead of resharing the take. Short form is not dying. The money moved. Two different claims, and only one of them survives contact with the rules.

## What actually changed on TikTok

TikTok banned nothing. It wrote a payout rule instead, and a payout rule does more work than a ban ever does.

TikTok's Creator Rewards Program pays on original content **longer than one minute**. A qualified view means someone watched at least five seconds. The video needs to clear 1,000 For You views, and it cannot be a Duet, a Stitch, or Photo Mode. The account behind it needs 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in the last 30 days.

Read that as a sentence about behavior rather than a policy. A 45 second video can do a million views and earn zero from the program. A 61 second video earns. Nobody took the short video away from anybody. They took it off the payroll.

## Reach and reward stopped pointing at the same video

The two numbers you optimize for now sit at different lengths, and that is the part almost nobody says out loud.

Buffer's analysis of 1.1 million TikToks found that 86% of everything posted runs under a minute, and that videos over 60 seconds pull 43.2% more reach and 63.8% more watch time than videos in the 30 to 60 second band. Socialinsider, looking at more than 6 million brand videos posted between January and June 2026, found the opposite curve for engagement rate: it peaks around 6% in the 15 to 30 second range and slides to roughly 4.2% to 5.5% once you cross a minute. Median views run the other direction, climbing with length and topping out past the two minute mark.

| What you want | Where it lives now |
|---|---|
| Engagement rate | 15 to 30 seconds |
| Reach and watch time | Over 60 seconds |
| Program payout | Over 60 seconds, no exceptions |
| Audience growth | Anywhere the hook lands |

Short gets you seen. Mid-form gets you paid. Anybody telling you to pick one is reading half the chart.

## Why a platform steers with the wallet instead of a rule

Because a rule makes news and a payout makes behavior.

Ban a format and you get a headline, a backlash, and a workaround by Thursday. Move the money and people comply without being told, then swear nobody made them do anything. The reward is the steering wheel, and it turns the boat without a single announcement that the boat is turning.

TikTok has used the other lever too. It ruled that live selling has to be a real person in real time, a real fence around a real format, and I wrote about that one in [The Live Line](/grimoire/the-live-line). Two levers, same platform, both aimed at raising the same floor. One is a rule you can read. The other you can only see by watching what gets paid.

Third-party trackers now put the average TikTok at roughly 42 seconds, up from about 25 seconds in 2022. No memo went out. The payout did.

## This is a floor raise, not a format funeral

For a small business, this is good news that showed up looking like bad news.

For years the price of entry on TikTok was volume. Post the disposable sub-minute clip, post it again, post it twice a day, out-churn everyone else in your category. I ran that treadmill for years, and I would run it again, but let me be honest about what it rewarded. Stamina, at least as much as substance. Most of what I made in that stretch was fine. Almost none of it needed to exist twice.

Kill the incentive for the throwaway and you get less of it. TikTok did not tell anyone to try harder. It stopped paying for the version that did not.

You do not have to out-churn the churn now. You have to make one thing worth a minute. Those are different jobs, and the second one favors the operator who knows something over the operator who can post through a Tuesday.

## What to run instead

1. **Keep making short.** Reach and discovery are not what changed. Cutting short form because of a payout rule is throwing away the thing that still works.
2. **Build mid-form on purpose.** Sixty to 180 seconds, planned as its own piece. Not a vlog, not a rambling cut with the good part buried at 2:40.
3. **Hook in three seconds regardless.** Length changed, attention did not. The second domino in [the sequence](/eight-dominoes) is still the hook that earns the first three seconds, and a longer video does not buy you a slower start.
4. **One idea per piece.** A minute is not a bundle. It is room to finish a thought that a 20 second clip could only gesture at.
5. **Read the payout terms yourself once a quarter.** Not the recap posts. The terms.

## The part worth keeping

There is no spell here. Every platform hands you a map, and most people read the wrong part of it. They read the announcements. The map is the payout.

TikTok moved its money to mid-form and raised the floor on the way through. I think that is the right call, and I am more curious about where they steer next than worried about what they steered away from.

So go find where your platform put its money this year. Then go make one thing worth a minute.
