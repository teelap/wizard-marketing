Agents are not killing your copy. They are splitting your market in two, and the half worth keeping now depends on whether your reason to care survives a machine's summary of it.

This stopped being a forecast. Google launched the Universal Commerce Protocol in January 2026 as an open standard for agentic commerce, built with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair and Target, and started rolling agentic checkout into Search and the Gemini app with merchants like Wayfair, Chewy and Quince. Microsoft runs Copilot Checkout. American Express shipped a developer kit and, in April, protection for purchases an authorized agent makes on your behalf. McKinsey estimates three to five trillion dollars of global retail could move through agentic commerce by 2030.

I have spent 13 years and two exits writing the message that closes the gap between the cheap option and the good one. That gap is where every margin I have ever protected lived. It is also the exact thing a machine is now standing in front of.

## Agentic buying sorts every product into one of two bins

Know which bin you are in before you write a word.

The first bin is commodities. Trash bags, sponges, a fourteen dollar hammer. Nobody agonizes over those, so the machine handles them, and they get agented into oblivion. Copy never decided that sale. It is not a loss.

The second bin needs taste. A product whose fit has to line up with the buyer's own judgment. The agent cannot close that one alone, so it narrows the field and brings the finalists back to a person. That half still runs on messaging. But the reader changed. You are not writing for the buyer anymore. You are writing for the thing that talks to the buyer.

## Agents do not buy the cheapest thing, they buy the most legible thing

The lazy assumption is that a machine optimizes to the lowest price. The 2026 evidence says otherwise.

Agents match structured product data against the request, then weigh price against availability, reviews, merchant trust and citations. Reporting through this year found that products without reviews get recommended at much lower rates even when the listing is otherwise complete, and that agents tend to favor stable, well documented listings over cheaper ones with erratic pricing or vague specifications.

That should change what you panic about. The threat is not that you are expensive. The threat is that you are illegible. A hundred and thirty dollar hammer with a clear, structured, well evidenced reason for existing beats a ninety dollar one the machine cannot parse. Being unpriceable is a strategy. Being unreadable is a death sentence.

## Citation is facts. Relay is feeling.

Everything the industry calls AI search optimization is about the first one. This is the second one, and almost nobody is working on it.

Getting cited means your facts survive. That is a solved enough problem: structure the page, publish real data, be [the brand that gets mentioned most](/grimoire/the-most-mentioned-wins). Relay is different. Relay is whether the reason to care survives being paraphrased by something that cannot care.

An agent can quote your specifications flawlessly and still flatten your [Core Attraction](/grimoire/core-attraction) into a bullet list. You end up accurately described and unpersuasive. Most copy written today reads beautifully to a human and collapses into specs and a price the moment a machine digests it. That collapse is the new failure mode, and it does not show up in any dashboard you look at.

## What survives a summary and what gets stripped

The pattern is consistent once you start looking for it.

| Gets stripped | Survives |
| --- | --- |
| Tone, rhythm, clever lines | A named mechanism the agent has to repeat |
| "Premium," "crafted," "best in class" | A specific, checkable claim |
| Emotion carried by phrasing | Emotion carried by a fact |
| Your brand story as narrative | Your brand story as a reason for a design choice |
| Vague superiority | A stated tradeoff you chose on purpose |

Read that table as one instruction. Anything whose persuasive weight sits in the wording will not make the trip. Anything structural will. If the only thing separating you from a competitor is that you said it better, a machine is about to erase that advantage on your behalf.

So stop hiding your differentiation in adjectives and put it in nouns. Name the mechanism. State the tradeoff. Attach the feeling to a fact that has to be carried, because a fact is the only cargo the relay is guaranteed to deliver.

## Run the relay test this week

Do this before you rewrite anything, because the result will tell you what to fix.

1. Take your main product or service page exactly as it stands.
2. Paste it into a chat assistant and ask it to recommend you to a specific buyer with a specific budget and a named competitor.
3. Read the output as if you were that buyer and had never seen your page.
4. Mark everything the machine dropped. That is the copy doing no load bearing work.
5. Rewrite so the reason to choose you is a structural claim, not a well turned phrase.

I have not run this against a hundred brands and I am not going to pretend I have. Run it against your own page and you will know inside ten minutes whether your differentiation is real or whether it was decoration the whole time.

The interface is moving. A layer now sits between your buyer and your shelf, and it reads your copy before they do. There is no spell for that. There is only writing something true enough and specific enough that a robot with no taste is still forced to carry yours.
