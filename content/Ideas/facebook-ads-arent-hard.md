## The hook

> Facebook ads aren't for the faint of heart, but they're not supposed to be hard. Most beginners lose on four avoidable mistakes.

---

## The core idea

Facebook ads feel like a minefield because the pros have them on lock and aren't telling you what to do. But most of what sinks beginners is four fixable mistakes: spending too little, targeting by income instead of behavior, picking the wrong campaign objective, and a mismatch between the ad and the page it sends people to. Fix those, let the creative do the heavy lifting, and accept that the goal is to fail fast and learn.

---

## The tension

Control versus letting the machine learn. Beginners want to control everything, tight budgets, narrow targeting, hand-picked audiences, because it feels safe. That instinct is exactly what breaks the ads.

Spending too little is worse than spending too much, because Facebook can't optimize on scraps of data. And obsessing over targeting misses that the platform builds the targeting for you once the creative is strong. The move isn't tighter control. It's feeding the machine enough budget and good enough creative that it can do its job, then getting out of the way.

---

## Why it matters

The four mistakes are quiet budget-killers, and they're all avoidable before you spend a dollar. Underfunding starves the algorithm. Chasing "high-income" audiences targets people who often won't buy, when targeting by activity or affiliation, the brands and behaviors that signal a real fit, works far better. Picking the wrong objective wastes the one setting you only get to choose once. And sending a "rubber ducky" ad to a "rubber moose" page breaks the trust the click was built on.

Get those right and Facebook becomes what it's supposed to be: a machine that finds your buyers if you feed it good creative and enough room to learn. The winners aren't the ones with the cleverest targeting. They're the ones who stopped making the four mistakes and let the creative carry it.

---

## Building blocks

1. **Spend enough to let it optimize.** Spending too little is worse than spending too much, because Facebook needs data to learn. Starve the ad set and you never give the algorithm a chance. Fund it enough, across one or two ad sets, that the machine can actually find your people.

2. **Target by behavior, not by income.** Just because someone has money doesn't mean they'll buy, and high earners are often frugal. Target by activity and affiliation instead: the brands they follow, the things they do. Selling a bath product? The people who shop the obvious bath-and-body brands are a better signal than a zip code.

3. **Pick the right objective the first time.** You choose your campaign objective once, and it shapes everything after. If you want sales, choose sales. Most of the time the answer is leads or sales, not the vanity objectives that get you cheap clicks and no customers.

4. **Match the ad to the page.** If the ad shows a rubber ducky, the landing page better show a rubber ducky, not a rubber moose. The promise that earned the click has to be the first thing they see on arrival, or you've paid for a visitor you immediately confused.

5. **Creative beats targeting, so fail fast.** Facebook figures out the targeting if the creative is killer, so put your energy there, and test relentlessly. Keep your top performers, write new ones against them every couple of weeks, and remember it takes around fifty conversions before you even know how an ad runs. The goal is to fail, and fail fast, so you learn quickly. (And install the pixel today, even if you're not running ads yet, so the data's waiting when you do.)

## The pushback

- **"I can't afford to spend more."** Then you can't afford to spend at all, yet, because underfunding wastes the smaller amount completely. Better to wait and run one properly funded ad set than to dribble money into one the algorithm can never learn from.
- **"Income targeting seems obvious for a premium product."** It's the common trap. Money doesn't equal intent, and high earners are often careful spenders. Behavior and affiliation predict purchase far better than how much someone makes.
- **"Isn't 'fail fast' just burning money?"** It's spending money to buy information, on purpose, fast. Every losing ad tells you something true about your audience. The waste is running a loser slowly for months because you were afraid to test.
- **"Why install the pixel if I'm not advertising yet?"** Because it's collecting audience data now that you'll need later. The day you decide to run ads, the difference between a warm pixel and a cold start is months. It costs nothing to start gathering today.

## Where it leaves you

Look at your last underperforming campaign against the four mistakes and you'll usually find at least two of them. That's good news, because they're all fixable settings, not a talent gap. The next move is to fund one ad set properly, target it by behavior, match the ad to the page, and let the creative and the algorithm do the work you were trying to do by hand.
