## The hook

> Single-channel marketing is dead. If you want to show up everywhere, all the time, this is how. One omnichannel client ran about 17x ROAS.

---

## The core idea

Just doing SEO, or just running paid ads, isn't enough anymore. The move is omnichannel: take one idea and express it across every channel your customer touches, so the message is unified and the touchpoints stack. Demand-side platforms, DSPs, are how you do the paid version of that, showing up everywhere all the time. Run it right and the numbers get serious. One omnichannel program drove roughly 17x return on ad spend.

---

## The tension

One channel versus everywhere. One channel is simpler to run and easier to report, so businesses plant a flag on SEO or Facebook and call it a strategy.

But buyers don't live on one channel, and a single touchpoint rarely closes anyone. The brands that stick are everywhere the customer already is, saying the same thing in each place, building familiarity through repetition. Programmatic now accounts for the overwhelming majority of digital display, around nine in ten dollars. Showing up everywhere stopped being exotic. It became the default, and the businesses still betting on one channel are the exception, not the safe choice.

---

## Why it matters

Touchpoints compound. The customer who sees you on search, then a connected-TV ad, then a creator's feed, then a retailer's site doesn't experience four campaigns. They experience one brand that's everywhere, and everywhere reads as established. Omnichannel shoppers reliably buy more than single-channel ones.

And it's where the money is going. Connected TV and retail media are among the fastest-growing ad channels, and the DSP is the control panel for all of it. The point isn't to be on every channel for its own sake. It's that unified presence across many is what turns a single ad into a brand the customer already trusts by the time they're ready to buy.

---

## Building blocks

1. **Single-channel marketing is dead.** Just SEO, or just ads, leaves you invisible everywhere else the customer looks. Omnichannel isn't doing more random things. It's taking one concept and running it across every channel, so the message is unified and the touchpoints reinforce each other.

2. **DSP is how you show up everywhere, all the time.** A demand-side platform places you across the open web, apps, and connected TV from one seat. This is how DSP advertising works, but if you are a caveman: you decide who, the machine puts you in front of them everywhere they go.

3. **The 17x came from stacking, not spending.** The strong return wasn't a bigger budget. It was one message, placed across channels, hitting the same person enough times that the brand felt familiar before the ad ever asked for the sale. Frequency across surfaces did the work.

4. **You can run it in 15 minutes a day.** Omnichannel sounds like a full-time job. It's a checklist: one day for messaging, one for content, one for social, one for email, one for analytics. One idea, repurposed, scheduled, measured. The discipline is unification, not volume.

5. **Be everywhere, including off your own lawn.** Showing up everywhere isn't just paid. It's getting mentioned where buyers and now AI both look: Reddit, YouTube, the places that aren't your website. If you only exist on your own domain, the algorithms, and the customer, assume you're small. Presence across surfaces is how you become a name.

## The pushback

- **"I can barely run one channel well."** Then start with one and unify from there. Omnichannel isn't five half-built channels. It's one message you extend outward as you get each surface right. The mistake is five different messages, not one message in five places.
- **"DSP sounds like big-brand money."** It used to be. It isn't anymore. Programmatic is most of digital display now, and the platforms reach down-market. The barrier is knowing how to run it, not the size of the check.
- **"Isn't 'be everywhere' just spray and pray?"** Spray and pray is five disconnected messages. This is one message, deliberately placed where your specific buyer already is, repeated until it's familiar. The targeting is the opposite of spraying.
- **"How do I measure something this diffuse?"** By the return, not the channel. The 17x example measured what came back, not which touch got credit. Track the program, watch the blended return, and stop trying to assign the whole sale to the last click.

## Where it leaves you

List every place your best customer spends attention in a week, then mark how many of them carry your message. The gaps are where the brand disappears, and disappearing is what makes you forgettable when the buying window opens. The next move is to take one idea and start putting it everywhere they already are, the same message in each place, until familiar turns into trusted.
