## The hook

> Your product is fine. Your messaging probably sucks. Here are eight ways to frame any offer so people know why to care.

---

## The core idea

Most products are described, not framed. You list what it is and hope the value is obvious. Offer amplifiers are eight angles you can run any offer through to make what's special about it land: New, Only, Big, Fast, Easy, Safe, Predictable, Simple. Pick the one or two that are actually true for you, lead with them, and a flat description turns into a reason to buy.

---

## The tension

Describing versus framing. A description is honest and forgettable: "we make project management software." A frame tells the customer which door to walk through: is this the new thing, the only thing, the fast thing, the safe thing.

The product didn't change. The framing did. And framing is the part most businesses skip, because describing feels like enough and framing feels like spin. It isn't spin. It's choosing which true thing to say first.

---

## Why it matters

People don't buy what they can't categorize. When a customer can't tell why your thing matters in the first few seconds, they don't dig deeper. They leave. The amplifier is how you hand them the why on contact.

And it forces a decision you've been avoiding: what are you actually best at. You can't lead with all eight. Picking your one or two real amplifiers is a positioning exercise disguised as a copywriting trick.

---

## Building blocks

1. **The eight, in Jake's words.** New is what's hot: discover, unveiling, introducing. Only is exclusivity: exclusive access, patented, the in-crowd. Big is size: massive, grand, colossal. Fast is results: instant, rapid, accelerated. Easy is avoiding work: effortless, user-friendly. Safe is reassurance: rest easy, low-risk, trust. Predictability is no surprises: consistent expectations. Simple is no confusion: direct, streamlined, straightforward.

2. **Pick one or two, not eight.** An offer that claims to be new and only and big and fast and easy and safe is an offer that claims nothing. The discipline is choosing. Which two are true enough that you'd stake the headline on them.

3. **Match the amplifier to the buyer's fear.** A nervous buyer wants Safe and Predictable. A busy one wants Fast and Easy. A status buyer wants Only. The right amplifier isn't the one you like. It's the one that answers the thing keeping your customer from saying yes.

4. **Rewrite a flat line on the spot.** "We offer financial planning" is a description. "Predictable retirement income, no surprises" leads with an amplifier. Same service. One of them tells the customer which problem it solves.

5. **Amplifiers are the language layer on top of the real thing.** They don't replace a good product or an honest promise. They make the value legible fast. Run your offer through all eight as an exercise, keep the two that don't feel like a stretch, and throw out the rest.

## The pushback

- **"Isn't this just adjectives?"** Adjectives decorate. Amplifiers position. "Fast" as filler is noise; "Fast" chosen because speed is the one thing your buyer is desperate for is a strategy. The word is the same. The decision behind it isn't.
- **"My product is several of these at once."** Maybe. But the customer can only hold one or two on first contact. Leading with all of them is how you become background noise. Rank them and lead with the strongest true one.
- **"Won't this make me sound like a hype ad?"** Only if the amplifier is false. New that isn't new, Only that isn't exclusive, that's hype. An amplifier that's actually true reads as clarity, not hype.
- **"Where do I even start?"** Run the offer through all eight out loud. Two will feel obviously true and the rest will feel like a stretch. The two that fit are your frame.

## Where it leaves you

Take your homepage headline and ask which of the eight it's running on. If the answer is "none," that's why it reads flat. The next move is to pick the one amplifier that's truest for you and rewrite the first line around it, then notice how much sooner a stranger understands what you're for.
