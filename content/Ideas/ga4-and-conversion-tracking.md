## The hook

> When you market, you get two things: results and data. If you're not getting the data, you're wasting your money.

---

## The core idea

Marketing without measurement is gambling with extra steps. Every campaign hands you two things, results and data, and the data is what tells you what to do next. GA4 is the free tool that captures it, and yes, it's overwhelming if you're used to the old way. But you don't need all of it. You need to know the four report areas, set up conversion tracking, and actually look at what's working.

---

## The tension

Doing versus measuring. Running the campaign feels like the work, so the tracking gets skipped, "I'll set up analytics later." Then the campaign ends and there's no way to tell what happened.

If marketing doesn't return data, it failed, even if it sold something, because you can't repeat what you can't see. The discipline isn't running more campaigns. It's refusing to run any campaign you can't measure. The setup feels like overhead right up until the moment you need to know which half of your budget worked.

---

## Why it matters

Conversion tracking is the difference between "we got some traffic" and "this channel produced these specific actions." Mark the thank-you page as a conversion and suddenly every visit can be traced to whether it did the thing you wanted. That's the number that lets you double down on what works and cut what doesn't.

And the goal isn't to drown in dashboards. It's to see the few things that matter, where people come from, how they use the site, whether they convert, often enough to act on them. A rough report you actually read beats a perfect one you never open, which is exactly why getting it scheduled into your inbox matters more than getting it pixel-perfect.

---

## Building blocks

1. **No data, no marketing.** Every campaign returns results and data. If you're only collecting results, you're flying blind, and the data is the half that tells you what to change. Marketing that hands back no data has failed at its second, more important job.

2. **GA4 in four boxes.** Acquisition: where people come from. Engagement: how they use your site. Monetization: the ecommerce numbers. Retention: whether they come back. That's the map. Everything else is detail you can ignore until you need it. The tool is awesome and it takes time, so start with the four.

3. **Set up conversion tracking.** Pick your success page, the thank-you or confirmation page, create a custom event that fires when someone lands on it, and mark that event as a conversion. Now you're not guessing whether marketing worked. You're counting the exact action you cared about.

4. **Report so you'll actually look.** Don't make checking analytics a chore you skip. Use a prebuilt reporting template and schedule it to land in your inbox on a cadence. The report you're handed gets read. The dashboard you have to go find doesn't.

5. **Numbers tell you what, heat maps tell you why.** GA4 says people leave a page. A heat map or session recording shows you they scrolled halfway and bounced, so you know the content showed up too late. Pair the counting tool with a watching tool and you stop guessing at the reasons behind the numbers.

## The pushback

- **"GA4 is too confusing to bother with."** It's confusing if you try to learn all of it. Learn four report areas and one conversion setup and you've got ninety percent of the value. The confusion comes from treating it as a course instead of a few specific jobs.
- **"I can tell it's working from sales."** Sales tell you something happened. Data tells you why, from where, and whether you can repeat it. Without it, a good month and a lucky month look identical, and you can't tell which lever to pull again.
- **"Setting up tracking is a developer task."** Basic conversion tracking is a few clicks: a success page, a custom event, mark it as a conversion. You can do it yourself in the interface. The barrier is thinking it's hard, not the doing.
- **"I don't have time to stare at dashboards."** Then don't. Schedule the report to email itself to you and add a heat map so the data interprets itself. The point is to look rarely and act, not to live in the tool.

## Where it leaves you

Ask one question about your last campaign: could you prove, in numbers, what it produced? If the answer is a shrug, the data half never got captured, and you paid for results you can't repeat. The next move is to set up one conversion event on your most important success page, so the next campaign hands back the data this one didn't.
