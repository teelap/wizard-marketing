## The hook

> Turn one piece of content into thirty. One long-form, a few medium, a pile of short. Stop reinventing and start repurposing.

---

## The core idea

You don't need to make thirty pieces of content a week. You need to make one good long-form piece with several ideas in it, then break it down. One long-form becomes a few medium pieces, and each medium piece becomes seven to nine short ones. That's the 1-4-10 system: one big thing, multiplied, instead of thirty small things invented from scratch.

---

## The tension

Inventing versus multiplying. The feed demands constant content, so creators try to invent something new for every platform, every day, and burn out fast.

But it's far easier to make one big piece of content than a hundred little ones. The little ones should come out of the big one, not get conjured independently. Inventing thirty times a week is the grind that kills consistency. Multiplying one piece thirty ways is a system you can actually sustain, because the hard creative work happens once.

---

## Why it matters

Repurposing is how one hour of real thinking becomes a month of presence. The long-form piece is where the ideas live; everything else is reformatting. That's the difference between content as a treadmill and content as a system, and the system is the only version that survives a busy quarter.

It also keeps your message consistent across platforms. When every short piece traces back to the same long-form source, you're saying one coherent thing in many places, not thirty disconnected things. One idea, many native formats, all reinforcing each other, which is exactly how presence compounds instead of scattering.

---

## Building blocks

1. **Start with one long-form piece.** A podcast, an interview, a walkthrough, a white paper, anything with multiple concepts in it. This is the source. Plan it on purpose to contain at least three distinct ideas you can pull out and stand on their own, because those become everything downstream.

2. **Break it into a few medium pieces.** From the one long-form, make a handful of medium pieces, a blog post, a YouTube video, a newsletter, each one covering a single concept from the source in full. The medium tier is where one idea gets room to breathe on its own.

3. **Each medium becomes seven to nine short pieces.** From each medium piece, cut a stack of short ones: TikToks, Instagram posts, a quote graphic, a landing-page line, ad copy, a short clip. One concept, fully expressed once, then sliced into the formats each platform wants.

4. **One big piece is easier than a hundred little ones.** This is the whole reason it works. The grind isn't making content, it's inventing it from nothing, over and over. Make the one big piece well, and the thirty small ones are reformatting, not reinvention. Start a podcast and chop it up into everything else.

5. **Pair it with an idea engine so you never run dry.** The system multiplies a piece; a topic framework decides what the piece is about. Run your core subject through your subtopics and the eight angle types (the matrix that keeps angles from repeating), and you've got an endless supply of long-form sources to feed the 1-4-10 machine.

## The pushback

- **"Won't repurposing feel repetitive to my audience?"** Not if each piece is a different cut for a different platform. The same idea expressed as a TikTok, a blog, and a newsletter reaches different people in different moods. Repetition is posting the identical thing everywhere; this is one idea, natively formatted.
- **"I don't have time to make long-form content."** Long-form is the time saver, not the time cost. One good piece feeds a month. The alternative, inventing a fresh small piece every day, is the thing actually eating your time. Front-load the thinking, coast on the reformatting.
- **"My niche doesn't suit podcasts or video."** Long-form is any format with multiple ideas in it: a detailed post, a guide, a recorded talk. The medium doesn't matter. What matters is starting from one rich source instead of thirty thin ones.
- **"Cutting it all up sounds like a lot of work too."** It's reformatting, not creating, and most of it can be templated or automated. The hard part, having something to say, is already done. Slicing is the easy, repeatable half.

## Where it leaves you

Look at how you make content now: are you inventing each piece, or multiplying one source? If it's inventing, that's the burnout, and the cap on how consistent you can be. The next move is to make one long-form piece this week with three clear ideas in it, then practice cutting it down until the multiplication becomes a habit instead of a grind.
