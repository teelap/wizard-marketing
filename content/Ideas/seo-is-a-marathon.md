## The hook

> SEO is a marathon, not a sprint. Your website isn't a sign out front. It's a digital salesperson, and you have to keep it alive.

---

## The core idea

SEO doesn't pay off in a week and it doesn't stay paid off if you stop. It compounds with consistency and it decays without it. Your website isn't static signage that sits out front of the building. It's alive, a digital salesperson, and like anything alive it needs maintenance. Most SEO that "doesn't work" was just abandoned three months in, right before it would have started to climb.

---

## The tension

The hack versus the habit. Everyone wants the clever trick that ranks them tomorrow. So they chase hacks, see nothing in a month, and quit.

But SEO doesn't have to be hard or clever. It has to be consistent. You maintain a car by changing the oil, rotating the tires, replacing one now and then. The website is the same. Skip the maintenance and it doesn't hold steady, it slides, because the moment you stop feeding Google fresh signals, Google starts testing whether you still deserve the spot.

---

## Why it matters

Consistency is itself a ranking signal. Updating a little of your site regularly beats overhauling the whole thing once a year, because steady activity tells Google the page is tended and current. The slow, boring maintenance is the strategy, not the thing you do until the "real" strategy starts.

And the downside of stopping is real and visible. Pause a working SEO program and the rankings don't freeze in place. They drift back down, impressions fall off, and you watch months of gains erode. The compounding cuts both ways: it builds when you show up and it unwinds when you don't.

---

## Building blocks

1. **It's alive. Treat it like one.** Your website is a digital salesperson, not a sign bolted to the wall. It needs oil changes: updated old posts, fresh content, fixed pages. Maintenance isn't busywork. It's the signal that keeps you ranked.

2. **Consistency beats intensity.** Updating a little every week beats a giant overhaul every quarter. Google rewards the tended garden, not the occasional bulldoze. Small and steady is the whole game.

3. **Stop and it decays.** Pause a working program and rankings start sliding back, impressions drop, and the gains unwind. The same compounding that built your position will quietly dismantle it the moment you walk away.

4. **Three things set the timeline.** Keyword difficulty (how saturated the term is), domain authority (how much the internet already trusts you), and content relevancy (whether the topic fits your site). The uncommon term can rank in a week. "Financial advisor" takes a while. And if you do hardware reviews and post a recipe, it won't rank, so stay in your lane.

5. **Search Console is non-negotiable.** You can't run a marathon blind. Google Search Console is the requirement, not the nice-to-have. It's how you see what you rank for, what's close, and what the maintenance is actually doing.

## The pushback

- **"I did SEO for three months and saw nothing."** Three months is the warm-up, not the race. Most programs are quit right before the climb. The ones that win are boring and consistent past the point where the impatient ones gave up.
- **"Can't I just hit it hard once and coast?"** No, and that's the part nobody likes. Coasting is decay. Rankings slide the moment the maintenance stops. There's no version where you do it once and keep the position forever.
- **"This sounds like a way to bill me every month."** It's why most SEO is monthly, yes, because the work is genuinely ongoing. A site that isn't tended loses ground. You're not paying for a service, you're paying for the maintenance the rankings depend on.
- **"My competitor outranks me and barely touches their site."** Then they've got authority or age you haven't built yet, which is exactly the marathon. Out-consist them and you close the gap. Authority compounds for whoever keeps showing up.

## Where it leaves you

Ask whether your SEO is a project you finished or a system you run. If it's a project, the decay has probably already started, quietly, in the background. The next move is to set the smallest sustainable maintenance rhythm you'll actually keep, weekly beats heroic-then-abandoned, and to open Search Console so you can see the marathon you're actually running.
