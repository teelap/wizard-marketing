## The hook

> Stop guessing at your Google Ads. Google will hand you the winning ad copy for free, if you know where to look.

---

## The core idea

Don't waste your ad budget testing copy from scratch. Go to Google, pretend you're a customer, and search for what you'd buy. The ads sitting at the top are written by competitors who already spent real money figuring out what works. Write down everything in them and you've built a swipe file: a collection of proven angles, headlines, and offers you can learn from instead of paying to discover yourself.

---

## The tension

Inventing versus collecting. The instinct is to write your ads from a blank page, be original, find your own angle. So you spend budget testing guesses.

But originality is expensive when the market has already run the test. The ads still running are the ones that work, because nobody keeps paying for an ad that loses. Inventing from scratch means re-buying lessons your competitors already paid for. Collecting means inheriting them for free.

---

## Why it matters

The ads currently running are a live, market-tested research file, and it's sitting on the search results page right now. Competitors don't keep funding losers, so what survives is signal. You get to study the winners before you spend a dollar of your own.

And it compounds. Build the habit and you've got an ever-growing library of proven copy, offers, and hooks to pull from every time you write an ad or a landing page. You stop staring at a blank page and start remixing what already works, which is faster and cheaper than originality for its own sake.

---

## Building blocks

1. **The four steps.** Go to Google. Pretend you're a customer and type in what you want to buy. Look at the ads. Write down everything in them as potential lines for your own. That's it. Congratulations, you just built your own personal swipe file.

2. **What's running is what's working.** Advertisers don't keep paying for ads that don't convert. So the ad that's been up for months isn't luck, it's a winner someone already validated with their budget. Survival is the proof.

3. **Collect angles, not just words.** Note the offers, the guarantees, the hooks, the order they make their points. You're not copying a competitor's ad. You're learning the patterns the market has already rewarded, then making them yours.

4. **It feeds the landing page too.** The swipe file isn't only for ad copy. The promises and angles that win the click are the same ones that should greet the visitor on the page. Use it to keep the message consistent from ad to landing page.

5. **Build it before you spend.** This is the cheapest research in marketing and almost nobody does it. Spend twenty minutes collecting before you launch a campaign, and you start from proven angles instead of paying to rediscover them.

## The pushback

- **"Isn't copying competitors lazy or risky?"** You're not copying an ad. You're studying patterns the market rewarded, then writing your own. That's research, not plagiarism, and it's what every good copywriter already does.
- **"My business is unique. Their ads won't fit."** The specifics won't. The patterns will: which promises earn clicks, how offers get framed, what objections get pre-handled. Take the structure, supply your own substance.
- **"What if my competitors are running bad ads?"** The ones running for a while aren't, because budget kills losers fast. Weight what's persisted. If an angle has been up for months across several advertisers, that's the signal.
- **"This sounds too simple to matter."** It is simple, and most people still launch on guesses instead. Simple and skipped is exactly where the easy edge lives. Twenty minutes of collecting beats a week of paid testing.

## Where it leaves you

Before your next campaign, spend twenty minutes searching like your own customer and copying what the survivors are doing. That file is a cheaper, faster research department than any test budget. The next move is to write your ad from proven angles, then make sure the landing page keeps the promise the ad just made.
