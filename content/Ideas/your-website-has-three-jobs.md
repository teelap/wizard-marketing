## The hook

> Your website has three jobs: load fast, work on mobile, and answer three questions. Most sites fail at least one.

---

## The core idea

Before a website can be clever, it has to be functional, and the functional bar is three things. It has to load fast, because if it doesn't load fast you don't have a website. It has to work on mobile, because that's the version Google indexes and the device most of your visitors are holding. And it has to instantly answer what is this, why am I here, and what do I do next. Miss any of the three and the design on top of it doesn't matter.

---

## The tension

Fancy versus functional. Businesses pour budget into how the site looks and skip whether it loads, works on a phone, or makes sense in five seconds. The pretty site that fails the fundamentals loses to the plain one that nails them.

A slow, beautiful site is a closed store with a gorgeous window. A desktop-perfect site that breaks on a phone is invisible to most of the people trying to use it. Functional isn't the boring part you do before the real design. It's the part the design depends on, and the part most sites quietly fail.

---

## Why it matters

Speed and mobile aren't polish. They're the price of being seen at all. Google indexes the mobile version of your site, so a site that's an afterthought on phones is an afterthought in search. And visitors decide fast: a page that doesn't load quickly loses them before the content ever renders.

Clarity is the third job and the one people skip entirely. A visitor who can't tell what you do, why they're there, and what to do next just leaves. The fix isn't more copy. It's answering three questions plainly, talking about the customer instead of yourself, and giving every page an obvious next step.

---

## Building blocks

1. **Load fast, or you don't have a website.** Run your pages through Google's free PageSpeed tool and watch the two that matter: how fast the main content paints and how fast the page becomes usable. Serve images in modern formats, lazy-load what's below the fold, and stop letting a slow site lose visitors before they see a word.

2. **Mobile is the real version.** Google indexes the mobile version of your site, not the desktop one, and most of your traffic is on a phone. Build mobile-first and check every page on an actual phone. A site that's perfect on a laptop and broken on mobile is broken where it counts.

3. **Answer the three questions.** What is this, why am I here, what do I do next. A visitor should get all three in seconds. If they can't, no amount of design saves the page. Give every page an obvious "get off the bus" moment, the clear next step, and keep a contact path in reach everywhere.

4. **Talk about them, not you.** Lay out your copy on the eighty-twenty rule: most of it about the customer, a little about your features. The customer is the hero, not your product. A page that's all "we" loses to a page that's about "you," because the visitor only cares about their own outcome.

5. **Don't fail at setup.** Most sites lose before they start with avoidable mistakes: leaving the default admin username, messy date-based URLs instead of clean ones, no analytics installed, a theme that isn't mobile-friendly, or search-engine visibility still switched off from development. Fix the boring setup and you've cleared the bar most sites trip over.

## The pushback

- **"My site looks incredible, though."** Looking incredible is the fourth job, and it doesn't count until the first three are done. A stunning site that loads slow, breaks on mobile, or confuses people is a beautiful failure. Function first, then make it gorgeous.
- **"Speed is a developer problem, not mine."** It's a business problem with developer fixes. You don't have to write the code, but you do have to refuse to ship a site that loses visitors before it loads. Run the test and make speed a requirement, not an afterthought.
- **"My customers are on desktop."** Some are. Google still indexes your mobile version, and checking the analytics usually shows more phone traffic than owners expect. Mobile-broken is search-invisible regardless of who you think your audience is.
- **"Isn't 'what is this, why am I here, what next' too basic?"** Basic and constantly failed. Most homepages can't pass it. The fundamentals feel obvious precisely because they're easy to skip, and skipping them is what quietly loses the visitor.

## Where it leaves you

Open your site on your phone, time how long it takes to load, and try to answer the three questions as if you'd never seen it. If any of that goes badly, the design budget was spent on the wrong layer. The next move is to fix the fundamentals first, speed, mobile, clarity, because those are the jobs that decide whether anyone ever experiences the design at all.
