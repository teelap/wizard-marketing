## The hook

> Google Ads is built to spend all your money. A handful of default settings are how it wastes it. Your ads serve you, not Google.

---

## The core idea

Whatever platform you run ads on is trying to spend your entire budget every single day, because that's how it makes money. Google Ads ships with defaults that quietly do exactly that: broad match keywords, the display and search-partner networks switched on, no ad schedule, auto-applied "recommendations," and loose location targeting. None of them are malicious. All of them favor Google's revenue over yours. Flip a few settings and you stop lighting money on fire.

---

## The tension

Google's interests versus yours. The platform's defaults are optimized for spend, and they're presented as helpful, so most advertisers leave them on and assume the system is on their side.

It isn't, not by default. The recommendations are written to make Google money, not to make your ads efficient. Your ads serve you, not Google, and acting on that means treating the default settings as something to audit, not trust. The same platform that runs your ads is the one trying to spend all your budget, and those two facts are always in tension.

---

## Why it matters

A few settings are the difference between a campaign that converts and one that bleeds. Broad match keywords match you to searches you never wanted, the display network sprays your budget across the open web, and a missing ad schedule runs your ads at 3 a.m. when no buyer is awake. Each one spends real money on the wrong clicks.

Fix them and the same budget suddenly works harder, because every dollar goes to searches that actually fit, at times buyers are active, in places they actually are. You're not spending more. You're refusing to spend on the waste the defaults were quietly funneling money into.

---

## Building blocks

1. **Turn off the display and search-partner networks.** The display network is Google's banner-ad service, and for a search campaign it's almost always wrong: it burns through your budget on placements that don't convert. Switch it and the search-partner network off so your money stays on the searches you meant to bid on.

2. **Use phrase or exact match, not broad.** Google will push every keyword to broad match, which matches you to searches loosely related to yours, and that's a budget sink. If you know the terms your buyers actually use, set them to phrase match so you stop paying for clicks you never wanted.

3. **Set an ad schedule.** Your ads don't need to run twenty-four hours a day. Run them when your buyers are actually active, and pause the dead hours and days. A business-to-business account, for example, has no reason to spend budget overnight and on weekends.

4. **Mine negative keywords from the search-terms report.** Go to the search terms your ads actually triggered on and add the irrelevant ones as negatives. That report is a list of exactly where your money leaked, and every negative keyword you add is budget you stop wasting on the wrong search.

5. **Don't auto-pilot it for Google.** Don't auto-apply recommendations, they're written to make Google money. Set location targeting to people actually in your area ("presence"), not everyone who's merely interested in it. And don't sweat the optimization score; it pushes you to spend more, not to spend better. Your ads serve you.

## The pushback

- **"Broad match reaches more people, though."** It reaches the wrong people, expensively. Reach without relevance is just a faster way to spend your budget on clicks that never buy. Phrase match trades empty volume for searches that actually fit.
- **"The display network gets cheap impressions."** Cheap impressions that don't convert aren't cheap, they're waste at scale. For a search campaign, the display network is usually where budget goes to disappear. Turn it off and keep the money on intent.
- **"Google's recommendations are supposed to help."** They help Google spend your budget. Some are fine, many push more spend or broader matching. The point isn't to ignore all of them, it's to stop auto-applying them as if they were on your side.
- **"Running ads only certain hours means missing buyers."** It means not paying for hours when your buyers aren't there. If nobody converts at 3 a.m., spending at 3 a.m. is donating to Google. Match the schedule to when your customers are actually active.

## Where it leaves you

Open your campaign settings and check three things: keyword match type, whether the display network is on, and whether there's an ad schedule. Most accounts are leaking on at least one. The next move is to flip those defaults back toward your interests, then read your search-terms report and start adding the negatives that stop the bleed.
