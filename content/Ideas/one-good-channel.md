## The hook

> One good marketing channel beats five half-assed ones. Get one right. Then add the next. That's the whole growth model.

---

## The core idea

Most businesses spread themselves across five channels and do none of them well. SEO, ads, email, social, all running at half effort, all underperforming, all draining the same finite attention. The faster path is the boring one: pick one channel, give it everything, get it actually working, and only then add the next. One channel that converts is worth more than five that limp.

---

## The tension

Spread versus focus. Spreading feels safe. If one channel underperforms, the others might carry it, so businesses hedge across all of them at once.

But hedging across five at half effort is how you guarantee five mediocre channels. Attention is finite. Split it five ways and nothing gets the focus it needs to cross from "running" to "working." Focus feels riskier and it's actually the safer bet, because one channel at full commitment beats five at partial every time.

---

## Why it matters

A channel that works is an asset you can build on. A channel that half-works is a tax you keep paying. Get one to genuinely convert and you've got a foundation, a proven engine you understand, before you add complexity on top. Add channel two from a position of strength instead of stacking another half-effort on a pile of them.

It's also how you avoid burning out the team and the budget. Five channels at once exhausts everyone and proves nothing. One channel, full commitment, for a set period, gives you a clean read: did this work, yes or no. That's a decision you can act on. Five blurry signals isn't.

---

## Building blocks

1. **Five half-assed channels is the most common growth mistake.** It looks like diversification. It's actually dilution. Each channel gets a fifth of the attention it needed, so each one underdelivers, and the business concludes "marketing doesn't work" when the real problem was spread.

2. **Get one thing right. Then add the next.** Pick the single channel most likely to reach your buyer, and make it work before you touch another. Then add channel two, get it right, and keep going. That sequence is the most sustainable model there is.

3. **Commit 110% for a set period.** Half-effort can't tell you whether a channel works, so don't half-commit. Give it everything for a defined window. At the end you'll have a real answer instead of a maybe, and a real answer is what lets you decide.

4. **Don't wear yourself out.** Running everything at once is how teams burn out and budgets vanish with nothing to show. Focus protects your energy as much as your results. Get one thing right, then move on from there, instead of being mediocre at all of it forever.

5. **This is the path to omnichannel, not the opposite of it.** Showing up everywhere is the destination. One-good-channel-at-a-time is the road there. You build a real omnichannel presence by mastering one surface, then extending, not by launching five half-built channels and calling the mess "everywhere."

## The pushback

- **"Isn't one channel risky? What if it dies?"** One channel that works and that you understand is less risky than five you barely run. And you're not stopping at one. You add the second from strength, which is far safer than hedging across five you never proved.
- **"My competitors are on everything."** Are they good on everything, or present on everything? Present-everywhere at low quality loses to excellent-somewhere. Win one channel decisively before you envy their spread.
- **"Doesn't this contradict 'show up everywhere'?"** No. Everywhere is where you're going. One-at-a-time is how you get there without faceplanting. Master a surface, then extend the same message to the next. The omnichannel brand was built one good channel at a time.
- **"How do I pick the one?"** Where your buyer already spends attention, weighted by what you can realistically do well. The best channel in theory you can't execute loses to the good channel you can pour yourself into.

## Where it leaves you

Count the channels you're "doing" right now, then ask how many are actually working versus just running. If the honest answer is "none of them are great," that's not a marketing failure, it's a focus failure. The next move is to pick the one with the best shot, give it everything for a real window, and stop splitting your attention into pieces too small to matter.
