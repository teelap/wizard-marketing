## The hook

> AI didn't make you more valuable. It made the middle worthless.

---

## The core idea

AI made production free. The grunt work in the middle is gone. What's left is the part the machine can't reach: knowing what's good, and saying exactly why. Taste and clarity. That's the whole job now.

---

## The tension

The easy take is "AI makes everyone faster." The real one: AI deleted the part of the job you got paid for. The middle got cheap. The two ends got expensive.

This is the bell curve, again. My own line from the Dudek episode: when you use AI, you're accepting the center of the bell curve, and it takes a human to connect with humans. Taste and clarity are how you climb off the center. They're the human part. The part nobody can automate, because it isn't production. It's judgment.

And here's the part worth sitting in instead of dodging. Taste isn't free. You earn it making bad work and feeling why it was bad. So if newcomers skip the middle because AI does it, where does their taste come from? That's the most interesting question in the whole idea. Don't answer it too clean.

---

## Why it matters

If this is right, the thing worth investing in isn't tools or speed. It's judgment, and the ability to say what you want out loud. Most people are racing to get better at the cheap part. The value moved to the part they're ignoring. That changes what's worth learning, who's worth hiring, and what you practice on purpose.

---

## Building blocks

1. **The effort didn't shift. It split.** The middle got cheap. Both ends got expensive: knowing what to make, and knowing if it's any good. The productive person owns both ends. AI eats the middle.

2. **Taste isn't picking. It's judgment with hands.** A critic points at the good thing. Easy half. The new skill is taste plus the ability to steer the machine toward it and catch it when it drifts. Hardest system to debug isn't the model. It's knowing which lever moves the output closer to the thing in your head.

3. **Clarity is the keystone.** Taste is internal. You know it when you see it. Clarity is what makes it transferable. The machine can't read your taste. It acts on how clearly you name it. Vague in, generic out.

4. **Clarity is how taste gets taught.** This is the answer to the apprenticeship problem. Say why a thing works and you hand someone the judgment without the thousand bad drafts. Clarity is taste you can teach.

## The pushback

- **"So everyone's just an AI manager now."** No. A manager signs off. You direct. You name the miss. You know the lever that fixes it. That's craft, not approval.
- **"Taste dies with the last people who learned it the hard way."** The real risk. Clarity is the bet. Say why the work works and you move the judgment without the apprenticeship. Unproven. Still the bet.
- **"This only holds for writers and coders."** Mostly true. It fits creative and knowledge work, not surgery or a courtroom. Scope it honest. Don't sell it as a law of everything.
- **"Taste is just a word for opinion."** Opinion picks. Taste predicts what works and survives contact with the audience. One is a preference. The other is a track record.
