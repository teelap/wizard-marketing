## The hook

> Want a career in marketing that survives anything? Be a T. Know everything broadly. Be genuinely great at one thing.

---

## The core idea

The most valuable marketer isn't the generalist who knows a little about everything, and it isn't the specialist who knows nothing outside their lane. It's the T-shaped marketer. The top of the T is broad: you understand everything marketing can do. The stem of the T is deep: you're genuinely excellent at one thing. Breadth makes you useful in any room. Depth makes you irreplaceable in one.

---

## The tension

Generalist versus specialist, and why picking one ruins you. The pure generalist can talk about everything and execute nothing at a high level, so they're easy to replace. The pure specialist is brilliant in a vacuum and useless the moment the work touches anything outside their corner.

The T is the resolution, and most people refuse to build it because it's two hard things instead of one. You have to read widely enough to understand the whole board, and go deep enough on one skill that you're the person they can't route around. Comfort is picking a side. Value is holding both.

---

## Why it matters

Breadth is what lets you see the whole sequence, so your one deep skill actually fits the strategy instead of fighting it. The best SEO in the world who doesn't understand conversion just sends traffic to a page that leaks. The T-shaped version sends traffic that converts, because they can see past their own lane.

And depth is what makes you safe. In a market where the broad layer is getting automated fast, "knows a bit about everything" is the easiest thing to replace. The one deep, real skill, paired with the judgment to use it in context, is the thing that keeps you in the room.

---

## Building blocks

1. **The top of the T: know everything you can do.** Not master it. Understand it. SEO, paid, content, email, analytics, brand, CRO. Enough to see how the pieces connect and where your work fits. Breadth is context, and context is what makes a specialist strategic instead of narrow.

2. **The stem of the T: be really good at one thing.** Pick the one skill you'll go deep enough on to be the obvious choice. Depth is what gets you hired and what gets you trusted. Breadth opens the door; depth is why they keep you in the room.

3. **Breadth without depth is replaceable.** The marketer who knows a little about everything and a lot about nothing is the first one automated, outsourced, or passed over. Generalist is a starting point, not a destination.

4. **Depth without breadth is brittle.** The specialist who can't see past their lane builds beautiful work that doesn't fit the strategy. They optimize their piece while the sequence around it breaks. Great at one thing, blind to the other seven, is a liability with a skill.

5. **It's a hiring lens, too.** If you're building a team, the T is what you're looking for in each seat: deep enough to own the function, broad enough to plug into the rest of the machine. A team of pure specialists doesn't add up to a strategy. A team of T's does.

## The pushback

- **"Isn't broad-and-deep just asking for everything?"** It's asking for two specific things, in order: wide enough to understand the board, deep enough to own one square. Not mastery of all of it. Most people fail by trying to be a flat line, all breadth or all depth.
- **"AI can do the broad layer now. Why learn it?"** Because the broad layer is how you direct the tools and judge their output. Without it, you're a deep skill with no idea where to point it. Breadth is exactly the judgment AI doesn't have.
- **"Which one deep skill should I pick?"** The one you'd still find interesting after a thousand reps, that the market actually pays for. Interest survives the grind. Demand pays the bills. Pick where they overlap.
- **"I'm a founder, not a marketer. Why do I care?"** Because it's your hiring rubric. Hire T's, not flat lines. The specialist who can't see the whole picture and the generalist who can't execute will both cost you more than they make.

## Where it leaves you

Draw your own T honestly. Is the top wide enough to see the whole board, and is there a stem deep enough that someone would hire you for it specifically? If you're a flat line either way, you know which direction to build. The next move is to pick the one skill worth going deep on, and start reading wide enough to use it in context.
