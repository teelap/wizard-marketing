## The hook

> Every page on the internet has one of four jobs. Build the page for its job, or it does none of them.

---

## The core idea

Every page has an intent: navigational, commercial, informational, or transactional. Navigational pages route people onward. Commercial pages separate your product from the alternatives. Informational pages answer a question. Transactional pages get the person to act. Build a page without knowing which job it's doing and it does all of them badly, which usually means it converts nobody.

---

## The tension

A page versus its purpose. Most pages get built by topic, "we need a page about X," with no decision about what the visitor is supposed to do once they land. So the page describes the topic and forgets to have a job.

But a page that doesn't know its job can't do it. An informational page that pivots into a sales pitch loses the person who came to learn. A transactional page buried in explanation loses the person who came to buy. Topic tells you what the page is about. Intent tells you what the page is for, and for is what determines whether it works.

---

## Why it matters

When the page knows its intent, every element on it has a reason to exist or get cut. The informational page answers the question cleanly. The transactional page removes everything between the visitor and the action. You stop decorating and start building toward one outcome, which is the outcome the visitor came for.

And the transactional pages are where the money literally is. Get an informational page slightly wrong and you lose a little patience. Get a transactional page wrong and the visitor doesn't transact, doesn't convert, doesn't close, and doesn't help your business grow. Those are the pages that most deserve the obsession, and most often get treated like everything else.

---

## Building blocks

1. **The four page intents.** Navigational: a hub that routes people to the right place (a listing or services page). Commercial: a page that separates your specific product or brand from the rest of the market. Informational: a page that answers a question. Transactional: a page built for one action, a form, a purchase, a booking.

2. **Transactional pages are hypercritical.** This is where the business is won or lost. Get it wrong and the visitor will not transact, will not convert, will not close. Strip everything that isn't moving them toward the one action. These pages earn the most scrutiny and usually get the least.

3. **On an informational page, answer on the page.** If the page asks a question, answer it right there. Don't make the visitor click somewhere else to get the payoff. Extra navigation between the question and the answer confuses people and sends them back to the search results.

4. **Every page answers three questions.** What is this, why am I here, and what do I do next. A visitor who can't answer all three in a few seconds leaves. The three questions are the universal test, on top of whichever of the four intents the page is built for.

5. **Service pages are pages, not blogs.** A page built to convert for a specific service should be structured as a page, with the path to act built in, not as a blog post that informs and trails off. Match the structure to the transactional job, and build one for each service you offer.

## The pushback

- **"My page covers a topic thoroughly. Isn't that enough?"** Thorough isn't the same as purposeful. A page can cover everything and accomplish nothing because it never decided what the visitor should do. Pick the intent, then cut everything that doesn't serve it.
- **"Isn't this the same as search intent?"** Related, and not the same. Search intent is about matching a keyword to the right kind of content. Page intent is about building the page itself to do one job once the visitor arrives. One gets them there, the other converts them.
- **"Can't one page serve multiple intents?"** Rarely well. A page trying to inform and sell at once usually does both at half strength. Let the informational page inform and link to the transactional one. Separate jobs, separate pages.
- **"My informational pages aren't supposed to convert."** They're supposed to answer cleanly and point to the next step. Even an informational page has a job and a what-do-I-do-next. None of them get to be aimless.

## Where it leaves you

Click through your own site and, on each page, say the one job it's doing out loud. The pages where you stall, or name two jobs, are the ones quietly underperforming. The next move is to give each page a single intent and rebuild it to do that one job cleanly, starting with the transactional pages, because those are the ones costing you money when they're vague.
