## 2. VOICE GUIDE — 15 traits with receipts

1. **Diagnosis-first, two-beat sentences.** Short claim, shorter twist.
   Signature: *"Your product isn't the problem. Your sequence is."* Also:
   *"Sequence is the problem."* Copy should diagnose before it sells.
2. **Fragments as percussion.** Periods where commas would be. *"No fluff.
   No drip sequence."* / *"Real strategies, real numbers."* / *"In sequence.
   Every time. No skipping ahead."* Never let a sentence carry three clauses.
3. **Tricolon with a kicker.** *"Find the bottleneck, fix the sequence,
   compound the results."* / *"He showed up, told the truth, and left
   something worth using."* Three verbs, escalating, concrete.
4. **The wizard skin wraps anti-magic substance.** Fantasy nouns name the
   *containers* (Arcanum, The Order, "Summon the Wizard," "This spell
   fizzled," "See the arsenal," castle/owl/candle scene) — but the *message*
   is always "there is no magic, only sequence/work/scars." Never use magic
   to explain results; use it to decorate the room. His agency-era channel
   bio: *"Forged in the fires of Mt. Doom, a marketing agency like never
   before."* [YouTube channel description]
5. **Numbers are the adjectives.** $100M+, 2 exits, 110K followers, 300K
   copies, 3→9 crews, 1.2%→6.5%, 17x ROAS, 48-hour reply SLA, $400/$750/
   $2,500/$5K-mo pricing in plain sight. When copy feels weak, add a number,
   not an adverb.
6. **Anti-fluff oath.** Recurring vocabulary: *no fluff, zero filler, no
   theory, no drip, raw truths, "calls out the BS," "done with theory."*
   Banned: corporate jargon (synergy, best-in-class, leverage-as-noun),
   hype superlatives without a number, exclamation marks (the site uses
   essentially none).
7. **Scars/trenches frame for credibility.** *"Built with scars"* (footer),
   *"Scars from the Trenches"* (timeline), *"earned his insights through
   bruises, not blog posts,"* *"I didn't learn marketing in a classroom.
   I learned it in the trenches."* Failure tuition = authority.
8. **Truth-teller-against-the-industry positioning.** *"Built 100K+ followers
   by showing up and telling the truth."* / *"Called out the industry's worst
   habits in public. Did it without permission."* / *"Followers who stayed
   for the truth, not the pitch."* CTA literally says **"Send Truth."**
9. **Systems/debugging metaphors from his IT past.** *"Twenty years of
   marketing taught me the hardest system to debug is people."* / framework
   as "operating system." Engineering language grounds the wizard shtick.
10. **Velvet rope honesty.** *"Jake doesn't work with everyone."* / *"THIS IS
    NOT FOR EVERYONE."* / *"Jake turns down applicants. Not out of ego."* /
    *"If you want validation for decisions you've already made, Jake isn't
    the right call."* Qualification copy does double duty as social proof.
11. **Personal-touch SLAs.** *"Jake reads every message and replies within
    48 hours."* / *"No automated responses. No assistant. Jake reads these
    himself."* Every form has a named human promise attached.
12. **Aphorisms — his actual quotable lines (use verbatim, attribute):**
    - *"People browse intellectually and buy emotionally."* [Dudek ep.]
    - *"When you use AI, you're accepting the center of the bell curve…
      it takes a human to connect with humans."* [Dudek ep.]
    - *"We focus on revenue as our core metric. If the needle doesn't move,
      was there really value?"* [CanvasRebel]
    - *"Anyone can get likes, hearts, shares, views, and clicks."* [CanvasRebel]
    - *"They can't take that audience and that awareness away from you."*
      (on personal brand surviving a business exit) [CanvasRebel]
    - *"Marketers need to make a return to people doing business with
      people."* [Videosocials]
    - *"SEO is evolving, not dead."* [Design Source Media]
    - *"Content that doesn't convert is noise."* [site timeline]
13. **Pop-culture nerd humor (video voice, underused on the site).** Episode
    titles: *"Need it or Yeet it: Marketing KPIs"*, *"Yer a Hero, Harry! |
    The Customer's Hero Journey"*, *"Hold My Beard"* itself (beer-pun +
    actual beard), explaining DSPs "but if you are a caveman," GSC's
    Validate Fix button compared to *"the infamous 'close elevator door'
    button."* Mt. Doom, Harry Potter, John Cena bits. Board-game-designer
    nerd cred is real (Grifters).
14. **TikTok caption voice = lowercase, plainspoken teacher.** Real captions:
    *"but you want a career in marketing. this is the first thing you should
    do."* / *"if you want to show up everywhere all the time, this is how
    you do it."* / *"this is how DSP advertising works, but if you are a
    caveman."* Direct second person, zero hype, mild deadpan.
15. **Close with an order, not an ask.** CTAs are imperative and in-world:
    *Work with Jake · Join The Order · Summon the Wizard · Send Truth ·
    Invite Jake · See the arsenal · Book a Half-Day.* Avoid "Submit,"
    "Learn more," "Get started" (generic).

**Sentence-level fingerprint to imitate:** average sentence under 12 words;
one idea per sentence; second-person dominant; concrete noun > abstraction;
em-dashes rare; semicolons never; questions used sparingly and answered
immediately ("Was there really value?").

**What he NEVER does:** emoji on the website (YouTube descriptions yes, site
no) · hedging ("might," "could potentially") · guru promises ("10x your
revenue overnight") · fake urgency timers · apologizing for price ·
unattributed testimonials with full-name-and-headshot fakery · long bio in
first person plural ("we") — the site is "I/Jake," the agency was "we."

## 5. FACT AMMUNITION — stats & stories a wizard can weave in

Mapped to Jake's themes; every item carries its primary source. Items marked
⚑ were re-verified via search this week.

**The Domino / Sequence (book metaphor)**
1. ⚑ **A domino can topple another ~1.5× its size.** Physicist Lorne
   Whitehead, "Domino 'chain reaction'," *American Journal of Physics* 51(2),
   1983. His 13-domino chain started under 10mm tall, nudged with a wisp of
   cotton; the 13th was 64× taller; energy amplification ≈ 2 billion.
   **29 progressively larger dominoes** starting at 5mm would fell something
   the height of the Empire State Building (Smithsonian Magazine write-up).
   → Already used on the site; now citable.
2. **Compounding sequence math:** 1.5^29 ≈ 127,834× the starting height —
   why order matters more than effort. (Derivable from #1; present as
   arithmetic, not a study.)

**Domino 1 — Core Attraction (emotion buys)**
3. ⚑ Jake's own line pairs with the research: people decide emotionally and
   rationalize after — Antonio Damasio's somatic-marker work (*Descartes'
   Error*, 1994) found patients with damaged emotional centers can't make
   simple decisions despite intact logic.
4. **Peak-end rule:** people judge an experience by its peak and its end,
   not its average — Kahneman et al., colonoscopy studies, *Psychological
   Science* 1993. (Use for offboarding/follow-up copy.)

**Domino 2 — Attention Catalyst (hooks)**
5. ⚑ **The $300 Million Button.** Jared Spool (UIE): replacing a forced
   "Register" with "Continue as Guest" lifted a major retailer's purchases
   **45%**, **+$15M the first month, +$300M the first year**. The retailer
   had 45% duplicate registrations and ~160,000 password resets/day.
   (Spool, "The $300 Million Button," 2009; retailer widely identified
   later as Best Buy.)
6. ⚑ **Obama 2008 splash-page test:** "Learn More" + family photo beat the
   default by **40.6%** in signups → ~2.88M extra emails → ≈ **$57–60M**
   extra donations. Dan Siroker (campaign analytics director, later
   Optimizely founder). The button text alone ("Learn More" vs "Sign Up")
   was worth +18.6%.
7. **First impressions form in ~50 milliseconds** — Lindgaard et al.,
   *Behaviour & Information Technology* 2006: visual appeal judgments of
   web pages are made in 50ms and bias everything after.
8. **Users read at most ~20–28% of words on a page** — Jakob Nielsen,
   Nielsen Norman Group eyetracking, 2008. (Justifies the fragment style.)

**Domino 3 — Value Proposition (the promise)**
9. **"1,000 songs in your pocket"** — Apple's 2001 iPod line (Steve Jobs
   keynote): the canonical concrete-promise rewrite of "5GB MP3 player."
10. **M&M's "Melts in your mouth, not in your hands"** (1954) — Rosser
    Reeves, the man who coined the **Unique Selling Proposition** (*Reality
    in Advertising*, 1961). A product *limitation-turned-promise*.
11. **FedEx: "When it absolutely, positively has to be there overnight"**
    (Ally & Gargano, 1978–82) — promise stated as the customer's stakes.

**Domino 4 — Impact Chain (stakes)**
12. **Loss aversion:** losses hurt ≈ 2× as much as equivalent gains —
    Kahneman & Tversky, prospect theory, *Econometrica* 1979. Frame the
    cost of inaction, not just the upside.
13. ⚑ **Speed-to-lead:** firms that contacted web leads **within 1 hour were
    ~7× more likely to qualify them** than those waiting even an hour more,
    and >60× vs. 24h+ — Oldroyd/InsideSales study of 1.25M leads, *Harvard
    Business Review*, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," 2011. (Backs
    his 48-hour-reply promise — and his Calendly-not-email-tag stance.)

**Domino 5 — Mechanism (the named system)**
14. **The Economist decoy:** Ariely's MIT test — with a useless
    "print-only $125" decoy, 84% chose print+web $125; remove the decoy and
    68% chose the cheap option instead (*Predictably Irrational*, 2008).
    Three-tier pricing with an anchor works.
15. **Williams-Sonoma bread maker:** a $275 machine sold poorly until a
    $429 model appeared beside it — sales of the $275 one roughly doubled
    (Itamar Simonson's research, retold in Poundstone, *Priceless*, 2010).
16. ⚑ **Charm-pricing field test:** raising a dress from **$34 to $39
    INCREASED demand by a third**; $34→$44 changed nothing — Anderson &
    Simester, "Effects of $9 Price Endings on Retail Sales," *Quantitative
    Marketing and Economics*, 2003.
17. **Named-mechanism precedent:** Domino's Pizza grew on "30 minutes or
    it's free" — the guarantee WAS the mechanism (1979–1993; retired after
    liability suits — also a cautionary tale about promises with teeth).

**Domino 6 — Barrier Breakdown (objections)**
18. ⚑ **Social proof, measured:** the hotel-towel study — "Almost 75% of
    guests reuse their towels" beat the standard eco-message (44.1% vs 35.1%
    reuse; the same-room version hit 49.3%) — Goldstein, Cialdini &
    Griskevicius, *Journal of Consumer Research*, 2008.
19. **Foot-in-the-door:** homeowners who first took a 3-inch "Be a safe
    driver" sticker later accepted a billboard-sized ugly lawn sign **76% vs
    17%** — Freedman & Fraser, *JPSP*, 1966. (Why the email comes before
    the pitch.)
20. **The jam study (choice overload):** 24 jams drew more browsers (60% vs
    40%) but **6 jams sold 10× better** (~30% vs ~3% purchase) — Iyengar &
    Lepper, *JPSP*, 2000. (Why three offers, not nine.)
21. **FREE distorts choice:** Lindt truffle 15¢ vs Hershey's Kiss 1¢ → most
    chose the truffle; truffle 14¢ vs Kiss **free** → 69% flipped to the
    Kiss — Shampanier, Mazar & Ariely, *Marketing Science*, 2007.

**Domino 7 — Embracing Limitations (weakness as proof)**
22. **Pratfall effect:** a competent person who commits a small blunder is
    rated MORE likable — Aronson, Willerman & Floyd, *Psychonomic Science*,
    1966.
23. **Blemishing effect:** adding a minor negative ("the only downside…")
    after positives *increases* purchase likelihood when people aren't
    scrutinizing — Ein-Gar, Shiv & Sopher, *Journal of Consumer Research*,
    2012.
24. **Avis "We Try Harder"** (DDB, 1962): opened with the confession "Avis
    is only No. 2 in rent a cars." Avis swung from 13 straight years of
    losses to profit within a year; commonly cited as moving share from
    ~11% to ~35% over the following years (figures vary by source — use
    "went from losing money to profitable within a year" as the safe claim).
25. **VW "Think Small" / "Lemon"** (DDB, 1959) — self-deprecating honesty
    about an ugly little car; Ad Age's **#1 campaign of the 20th century**
    (1999 ranking).

**Domino 8 — New Opportunity (replace the category)**
26. ⚑ **The 95-5 rule:** only ~5% of category buyers are in-market in any
    quarter; 95% aren't — Prof. John Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, for
    the LinkedIn B2B Institute, 2021. Brand memory must be built BEFORE the
    buying window opens. (= his Share of Brand Voice argument, with a
    citation.)
27. **60/40 rule:** brand-building beats activation for long-term growth;
    optimal budget ≈ 60% brand / 40% activation — Binet & Field, *The Long
    and the Short of It*, IPA, 2013.
28. **De Beers "A Diamond Is Forever"** — written by Frances Gerety (a
    woman in 1947, the only woman at N.W. Ayer) — didn't compete on price;
    it invented a ritual. Ad Age's **slogan of the century**. US diamond
    sales rose from $23M (1939) to $2.1B (1979) over the campaign era.

**Proof-of-craft stories (general ammunition)**
29. ⚑ **Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like"** (Wieden+Kennedy,
    2010): brief asked for +15% sales; delivered **+107%** (body-wash sales
    in the final month of the push; +60% at three months), 1.4B impressions,
    and 180+ personalized reply videos in 48 hours — the first great
    "respond at the speed of culture" campaign.
30. ⚑ **Dollar Shave Club:** $4,500 launch video (March 6, 2012), written by
    founder Michael Dubin — crashed the site in the first hour, **12,000
    orders in 48 hours**, company sold to Unilever for **$1B** (2016).
31. **Schlitz beer:** Claude Hopkins toured the brewery, saw the (industry-
    standard) steam-cleaned bottles, and advertised the *process* nobody
    else bothered to claim — "purity" preemption took Schlitz from ~5th to
    contention for #1 within months (Hopkins, *My Life in Advertising*,
    1927; exact ranking varies by retelling — tell it as Hopkins' story).
32. **Ogilvy's Rolls-Royce headline** (1958): "At 60 miles an hour the
    loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock" —
    pulled from an engineer's test report. Ogilvy called it the best
    headline he ever wrote; the chief engineer reportedly sighed, "It's time
    we did something about that damned clock." (*Confessions of an
    Advertising Man*, 1963.)
33. **John Caples, 1926:** "They Laughed When I Sat Down At the Piano —
    But When I Started to Play!" (U.S. School of Music) — the ad that
    taught direct response story-selling (*Tested Advertising Methods*).
34. **John Wanamaker (attributed):** "Half the money I spend on advertising
    is wasted; the trouble is I don't know which half." (Attribution is
    folklore — flag it as "the old department-store line" if used.)
35. **Ogilvy on Hopkins' *Scientific Advertising* (1923):** "Nobody should
    be allowed to have anything to do with advertising until he has read
    this book seven times."
36. **Rhyme-as-reason:** rhyming aphorisms are judged more TRUE than
    non-rhyming equivalents — McGlone & Tofighbakhsh, *Psychological
    Science*, 2000. (License for his aphorism habit.)
37. **Pique technique:** panhandlers asking for **"37 cents"** got ~60% more
    compliance than asking for a quarter — Santos, Leve & Pratkanis, *J.
    Applied Social Psychology*, 1994. Odd-specific numbers stop the script.
38. **Scarcity, measured:** cookies from a jar of 2 were rated more
    desirable than identical cookies from a jar of 10 — Worchel, Lee &
    Adewole, *JPSP*, 1975. (Cohort caps on Arcanum: cite this.)
39. **IKEA effect:** people value things more when they helped build them —
    Norton, Mochon & Ariely, *J. Consumer Psychology*, 2012. (Why the
    book's "apply each chapter this week" framing sells.)
40. **Goal-gradient effect:** café customers buy faster as the loyalty card
    fills; a 12-stamp card with 2 free stamps beats a 10-stamp card —
    Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, *J. Marketing Research*, 2006. (Waitlist
    progress bars, onboarding checklists.)
41. **Cart abandonment averages ~70%** — Baymard Institute's running
    meta-average of 50 studies (cite as "Baymard's running average," check
    current figure before print).
42. **Von Restorff isolation effect** (1933): the one different item in a
    list is the one remembered. (The lone red CTA on parchment is doing
    psychology, not just design.)
