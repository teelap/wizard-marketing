## The hook

> A pen topples a skyscraper in 29 steps. Your marketing isn't failing on volume. It's failing on order.

---

## The core idea

A domino can knock over the next one half-again its size. Line up 29 at that ratio, start the chain with the tap of a ballpoint pen, and the last domino is taller than the Empire State Building. Marketing runs on the same physics: one precise message, in the right order, each beat multiplying the one before it. Most businesses don't have a volume problem. They have a sequence problem, and they keep paying to push harder on a chain that's out of order.

---

## The tension

Reach versus sequence. The whole industry sells you more: more leads, more ads, more posts. So you buy reach and bolt it onto a message that was never built to compound.

A bigger push on the wrong order is just a louder version of being ignored. The fix isn't more energy into the first domino. It's lining the dominoes up so the energy carries. You don't need to push harder. You need to push in order.

---

## Why it matters

You can be the best in your market and still be its best-kept secret. That gap, between how good the work is and how well the market understands it, is not closed with budget. It's closed with sequence.

Get the order right and reach finally pays off, because every dollar of attention lands on a message engineered to multiply it. Get it wrong and you can outspend everyone and still lose to a worse competitor who simply explains themselves better. The businesses that win aren't the ones with the best product. They're the ones with the best chain.

---

## Building blocks

1. **The physics is real.** Lorne Whitehead, 1983: a domino topples one about 1.5 times its size. Force amplification. Each fall releases more energy than it took to start it. Twenty-nine steps from a fingernail-sized tile to the Empire State Building. Order is not negotiable, and it never was.

2. **The gap is a sequence problem, not a volume problem.** "And yet, you're still the best-kept secret in your market." More reach on a broken order just buys you a wider audience for a message that doesn't land. Smarter beats harder.

3. **Same crew, same stone, different frequency.** The Chicago landscaper changed nothing about the work. Same crews. Same skills. They changed the story the work told, and went from three crews to nine, $1.2M to $10M+. The product didn't move. The chain did.

4. **Eight dominoes, in order.** Core Attraction, Attention Catalyst, Value Proposition, Impact Chain, Mechanism, Barrier Breakdown, Embracing Limitations, New Opportunity. Each one sized to topple the next. Skip one and the chain stops there.

5. **Components are fun. Sequence is the work.** Better hooks, sharper headlines, cleaner funnels: the parts are teachable, so the industry sells the parts. But a message is a sequence, and a brilliant hook in front of the wrong emotion is a match struck in the rain.

## The pushback

- **"Isn't this just a sales funnel?"** A funnel is the stages a buyer moves through. This is the order the message arrives in. You can have a clean funnel and still load the dominoes backwards, which is why the traffic shows up and nobody converts.
- **"Sounds like positioning with a costume on."** Positioning is one domino. This is the chain that carries it. Position all you want; if the emotion before it and the mechanism after it are missing, the position never gets believed.
- **"I don't have time to build eight steps."** You don't have time to keep paying for reach that doesn't compound. The chain is built once. After that, deployment is just dropping the right line into the right slot.
- **"My product is genuinely better. Won't that win on its own?"** The work doesn't speak. You speak, on its behalf. A worse competitor with a better chain is already eating the lunch your quality earned.
