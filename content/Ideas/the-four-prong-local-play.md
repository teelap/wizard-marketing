## The hook

> For a local service business, here's exactly where to start. Four prongs: your profile, your pages, your reviews, and a small daily ad drip.

---

## The core idea

A local service business doesn't need a complicated marketing plan. It needs four things, run together: a dialed-in Google Business Profile, a dedicated service page for every service, a steady flow of reviews, and a small daily ad spend to make people aware you exist. Get those four working and you blow up on the map, because they can't buy you if they don't know you're there.

---

## The tension

Everything versus the four that matter. Local owners either do nothing, hoping word of mouth carries them, or they try every channel at once and burn out. Both lose.

The answer isn't more channels. It's the four that compound for a local business specifically. Your profile is the most important asset you have, and most owners barely touch it. Skip the four to chase something shinier and you're invisible on the exact searches your buyers are running a mile from your door.

---

## Why it matters

Local intent is the highest-converting traffic there is. Someone searching "plumber near me" is ready now, and the four prongs put you in front of them at that exact moment: on the map, on a page built for their service, with reviews that make you the safe choice, reinforced by an ad that keeps you familiar.

And it compounds cheaply. The profile and the service pages are mostly free. Reviews cost a follow-up text. The ad drip is small, just enough to keep people aware. Four modest efforts, aimed at the same local buyer, beat one expensive campaign aimed at everyone, because they all reinforce the same thing: you're the obvious local choice.

---

## Building blocks

1. **Google Business Profile is the asset.** For a local business, your profile is probably your single most important marketing asset, and if you're not running one, you don't exist on the map. Load it up: more photos, more posts (a few times a week), service listings with your locations, and reviews. Do that and you blow up in the maps results.

2. **A service page for every service.** On your website, build a dedicated page for each service you offer, structured as a page, not a blog post. Air-duct cleaning is a page. Emergency plumbing is a page. Each one targets the specific thing a local buyer searches for, instead of burying every service in one vague "what we do."

3. **Go local with your keywords.** Use geo-keywords: your service plus the towns you serve. And add the city name to every testimonial and portfolio item, "a patio we built in Barrington," not just "a patio we built." Those small geographic signals expand the searches you show up for, town by town.

4. **For the love of God, get reviews.** Reviews are critical, and most owners don't ask. Build a simple routine to request them after every job. Reviews feed the profile, win the map, and make you the safe choice at the exact moment a stranger is deciding between you and the next listing.

5. **Drip a little ad spend.** Put a small daily budget, on the order of fifteen dollars a day, into Google and Facebook ads, just enough to keep dripping people into your world and making them aware. It's not a blitz. It's a steady reminder, because they can't buy you if they don't know you exist.

## The pushback

- **"I get all my work from word of mouth."** Then you're capped at how fast people happen to mention you. The four prongs put you in front of the buyers actively searching right now, who'd never reach you through word of mouth, at the moment they're ready to hire.
- **"Reviews feel awkward to ask for."** Most owners think so, which is why most have too few, which is exactly the opening. A simple after-the-job text removes the awkwardness, and reviews are the thing that wins the map and the buyer.
- **"Ads feel expensive for a small shop."** This isn't a blitz. It's a small daily drip to stay visible. The point is steady awareness, not a big spend, because the goal is to be the name they already recognize when they need you.
- **"Isn't this just basic SEO?"** It's the local version, aimed at intent that converts now. The map, the service pages, the reviews, and the drip all point at the same buyer. Basic and skipped is where the easy local wins live.

## Where it leaves you

Score yourself on the four prongs right now: profile, service pages, reviews, ad drip. Most local businesses are running one, maybe two. The next move is to start the one you're weakest on, the profile is usually the highest-leverage place to begin, and add the others until all four are pointing at the same buyer searching from a mile away.
