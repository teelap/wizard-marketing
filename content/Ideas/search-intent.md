## The hook

> Every keyword carries an intent. Match the page to the intent, or you rank for nothing.

---

## The core idea

Every search falls into one of four intents: navigational, informational, commercial, or transactional. Google's whole job is to give the searcher value as fast as possible, so it ranks the page that matches what the searcher actually wants to do. Once you can read the intent behind a keyword, you instantly know what kind of page to build for it, and most SEO that fails, fails because the page answered the wrong intent.

---

## The tension

Keywords versus intent. Most people pick a keyword by volume, write a page, and hope. They treat the keyword as the target and the page as the answer.

But the keyword isn't the target. The intent behind it is. "Best CRM" and "buy CRM" share a topic and want completely different pages, and Google knows the difference even when the writer doesn't. Chase the word without reading the intent and you build a page that's technically about the keyword and useless for the person searching it.

---

## Why it matters

Match the intent and you stop guessing why pages don't rank. A guide answers an informational search. A service page answers a transactional one. Put the right page in front of the right intent and Google has an easy decision, because you gave the searcher exactly what they came for, fast.

Get it wrong and no amount of optimization saves you. A sales page aimed at a "how to" query competes against guides and loses, because it's answering a question nobody asked. Intent isn't a detail on top of SEO. It's the thing that decides whether the page had a chance at all.

---

## Building blocks

1. **The four intents.** Navigational: they want a specific place ("Jake the Wizard site"). Informational: they want to learn ("how to do local SEO"). Commercial: they're researching before buying ("best landscaper near me," comparisons, reviews). Transactional: they're ready to act ("buy," "pricing," "book now").

2. **The intent decides the format.** Informational wants a guide or how-to. Commercial wants a comparison or a "best of." Transactional wants a clean service or product page with the path to buy. Same topic, different intent, different page. Match the container to the intent.

3. **Google's job is speed-to-value.** Google's objective is to hand the searcher value as fast as possible. It ranks the page that does that. So the question isn't "did I use the keyword enough." It's "does this page do, instantly, what the searcher came to do."

4. **Intent mismatch is the silent killer.** Write a sales page for an informational query and you'll never rank, because you're competing for attention the searcher didn't come to give. The page isn't bad. It's pointed at the wrong job.

5. **Read the intent before you write a word.** Before you build the page, name the intent of the keyword. That one step tells you the format, the angle, and what "winning" even looks like for that search. Skip it and you're optimizing a page that was aimed wrong from the start.

## The pushback

- **"I just target high-volume keywords."** Volume without intent is traffic that bounces. A high-volume informational keyword sends learners to your sales page, and learners don't buy on contact. Match intent and lower-volume keywords often convert far better.
- **"Isn't this overcomplicating SEO?"** It's the opposite. Four buckets is the simplification. Once a keyword is sorted into one, the kind of page to build stops being a guess and becomes obvious.
- **"My page covers everything about the topic."** Then it serves no intent cleanly and competes against pages that each serve one. Google ranks the focused answer over the everything-page, because the focused one matches faster.
- **"How do I tell which intent a keyword is?"** Search it and look at what already ranks. Google has already decided the dominant intent, and the top results show you. If they're all guides, it's informational. If they're all product pages, it's transactional.

## Where it leaves you

Take your five most important keywords and label each with one of the four intents, then check whether the page you built actually serves it. The mismatches are the pages that will never rank no matter how much you optimize them. The next move is to rebuild each page for the job its searcher actually came to do.
