## 1. Format cards

### The Two-Beat Diagnosis — Tier A
- **What:** Jake's short-claim / shorter-twist, as a standalone single.
- **Why it works here:** singles carry the highest median ER on X (~3.56%, per x.md); the turn creates a dwell beat and bait for "no, actually…" replies (weighted ~13.5x). Text-only is the one format on X where text beats video.
- **Skeleton:** [confident wrong belief]. [the correction, shorter]. → optional third line that names the fix.
- **Hooks that fit:** The Reframe, The Contrarian Claim, The Callout.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** "Your funnel isn't broken. Your message is." · "A new logo won't fix slow sales. The logo was never the problem. Fix the sequence."
- **Don't:** explain it in a third sentence. The twist IS the post. No hashtags. No setup throat-clearing ("Here's the thing:").
- **Best for:** Myth, Pitfall, Takeaway.

### Stop / Start — Tier A
- **What:** a single that kills one habit and hands over its replacement in one breath.
- **Why it works here:** the imperative is a pattern-interrupt that stops the scroll, and the implicit "you're doing it wrong" earns corrective replies (~13.5x) and bookmarks people save as a rule (~10x). Lands inside the 71–100 char hot-take sweet spot from x.md.
- **Skeleton:** Stop [common move]. Start [the better move]. → optional fragment naming the payoff.
- **Hooks that fit:** The Blunt Imperative, The Contrarian Claim.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** "Stop describing your service. Start naming the wound it closes." · "Stop chasing more traffic. Start fixing what happens when it lands."
- **Don't:** stack three start/stops in one tweet. One swap. Don't soften it to "maybe try." Velvet rope, not a suggestion box.
- **Best for:** Tip, Pitfall, Method.

### The Receipt — Tier A
- **What:** a client result told as a one-tweet story, the number doing the proving.
- **Why it works here:** specificity and a real outcome make it bookmark-bait (~10x) and quote-repost-bait (~20x); "numbers are the adjectives" is Jake's voice and the stat reads as proof, not flex. Concrete beats abstract for dwell.
- **Skeleton:** [client/situation, third person]. [what changed — one lever]. [the result, as a stat-bank number]. → optional one-line lesson.
- **Hooks that fit:** The Receipt/Proof, The Story Open, The Stat.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** "A client changed one button. Conversion went from 1.2% to 6.5%. The traffic was never the problem." · "A landscaper went from 3 crews to 9. Same market. Better message. 1.2M to 10M-plus."
- **Don't:** make it a first-person flex ("I made a client 10M"). Frame it as the client's. Never invent a number — stat bank only. No victory-lap adjectives; the number is the adjective.
- **Best for:** Story, Method, Takeaway.

### The Reply-Bait Question — Tier A
- **What:** one sharp question that costs nothing to answer and invites a confession.
- **Why it works here:** replies are weighted ~13.5x and an author-reply-to-a-reply is the single biggest signal on the platform (~150x a like, per the source-code analysis x.md draws on). A low-friction question manufactures that thread. Reply to answers within 2–3h to beat the ~6h decay.
- **Skeleton:** [specific, low-stakes question about their own work]. → optional "I'll go first." with Jake's own answer.
- **Hooks that fit:** The Question, The Identity Callout.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** "What's the last line of copy that actually stopped your scroll. I'll go first." · "Founders. What's the one metric you'd keep if you lost all the others."
- **Don't:** ask something Googleable or yes/no. Don't ask and ghost — the reply-to-replies IS the play. No "thoughts?" filler.
- **Best for:** FAQ, Commentary, Takeaway.

### The Contrarian One-Liner — Tier A
- **What:** a single flat, quotable line that picks a fight with the consensus.
- **Why it works here:** polarizing takes drive reposts (~20x) and "well actually" replies (~13.5x); a clean universal line is built to be quote-reposted with the reader's own hot take attached. Deadpan brevity fits the 71–100 char band.
- **Skeleton:** [one declarative sentence that inverts a popular belief]. No hedge. No follow-up.
- **Hooks that fit:** The Contrarian Claim, The Reframe.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** "Most bad marketing isn't bad. It's invisible." · "Content that doesn't convert is just noise you paid for."
- **Don't:** explain or qualify it. The confidence is the format. No "in my opinion." No thread bolted on — if it needs a thread, it's a different card.
- **Best for:** Myth, Commentary, Takeaway.

### The Steal-This Tip — Tier B
- **What:** one tactic the reader can run today, stated as an order with the payoff named.
- **Why it works here:** immediately useful = high bookmark rate (~10x), the "save for later" signal X reads as long-term value. "Do this" outperforms "here's what." Situational because it needs a genuinely concrete, do-it-now lever.
- **Skeleton:** [the action, as an imperative]. [the test or trigger that proves it]. → optional one-line why.
- **Hooks that fit:** The Blunt Imperative, The Bold Promise.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** "Read your headline out loud. If you stumble, your customer already left." · "Rewrite your top line as the wound, not the service. Ship it before lunch."
- **Don't:** give a vague principle dressed as a tip ("focus on value"). It has to be executable in one sitting. No hashtag stuffing. No "pro tip:".
- **Best for:** Tip, Method.

### The Diagnosis Thread — Tier A
- **What:** a contrarian one-line diagnosis that opens into the fix, one beat per tweet. The flagship X move for teaching the messaging sequence.
- **Why it works here:** threads pull ~3x engagement and are the best follower-growth format on x.md; the hook tweet must stand alone (most threads live or die on tweet 1), and a tight per-tweet payoff drives dwell + bookmarks. Put the link in the FIRST REPLY (the relevant Grimoire post or the newsletter) — links in-thread cut reach 30–90%.
- **Skeleton (per tweet):**
  - **T1 (hook):** [confident wrong belief most founders hold]. [the shorter correction]. → one line that promises the fix below.
  - **T2:** name the real bottleneck. One sentence.
  - **T3:** why it stays hidden / why the obvious fix fails.
  - **T4:** the lever that actually moves it (the core fix).
  - **T5:** a stat-bank receipt or a composite client beat as proof.
  - **T6 (close):** the takeaway in one repostable line + soft CTA.
  - **First reply:** the CTA + link — the relevant Grimoire post or the newsletter, or the book "Buy the book" (CTA live, CONTENT_GUIDE §3).
- **Hooks that fit:** The Reframe, The Contrarian Claim, The Diagnosis.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** T1 — "Your product isn't the problem. Your sequence is. Here's the order most founders get backwards." … T6 — "Find the bottleneck. Fix the sequence. Compound the results."
- **Don't:** bury the diagnosis under a "1/" throat-clear. Don't cram two ideas in one tweet. Don't put the link in T1. Don't let any tweet run past ~250 chars.
- **Best for:** the messaging concepts (one per thread), Pitfall, Method.

### The Listicle Thread — Tier B
- **What:** a numbered promise in the hook, each item its own tweet, each item useful alone.
- **Why it works here:** the explicit count sets a completion loop that boosts dwell, and self-contained items get bookmarked (~10x) and screenshotted. Threads ~3x engagement. Higher-effort and more "content-y," so it's a B — use it to break up the diagnosis cadence, not as the default.
- **Skeleton (per tweet):**
  - **T1 (hook):** [number] [things] that [outcome the reader wants]. Most people do [number] of them.
  - **T2–TN:** one item per tweet — [the item, as a fragment]. [one line of why or how].
  - **Close:** "Save this. You'll need it on the next launch." + CTA in first reply.
- **Hooks that fit:** The Number Promise, The Bold Promise.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** T1 — "Eight things quietly killing your conversion rate. Most founders are guilty of three." … item — "Your headline names the service. Nobody buys the service. They buy the wound it closes."
- **Don't:** pad to hit a round number. Five real beats beat ten filler ones. Don't repeat the same idea reworded. No emoji bullets.
- **Best for:** Pitfall, Method, FAQ.

### The Teardown — Tier C
- **What:** Jake reads one real piece of marketing (an ad, a homepage, a campaign in the news) and diagnoses it live — as a punchy single or a short thread.
- **Why it works here:** topical + opinionated = reposts (~20x) and reply debate (~13.5x), and ~50% of For You reach comes from non-followers, so a take on something people already recognize travels. Experimental for this brand: it's reactive, needs a live subject, and risks punching at a named brand — pair with the daily-reactive pass, keep it about the work not the people.
- **Skeleton:**
  - **Single:** [the thing]. [what it gets right, one beat]. [the one thing that kills it]. → the fix in a fragment.
  - **Thread:** T1 names the piece + the verdict in one line → T2–T4 one flaw or win each → close with the transferable principle + CTA in first reply.
- **Hooks that fit:** The News React, The Callout, The Reframe.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** "This homepage is gorgeous. It also never says what they sell. Pretty isn't a value prop." · "Everyone loved that ad. It moved zero product. Awareness you can't convert is a costume."
- **Don't:** dunk on a small business or a person. Teardown the choice, not the human. Don't fake a take on something Jake hasn't actually looked at. No subtweeting without the receipt.
- **Best for:** Commentary, Myth, marketing-news (*Hold My Beard* mode).

## 2. Hook bank

The first line of a single and the first tweet of a thread. The cure for flat openings — diagnosis-first, fragmented, never a wind-up.

1. **The Reframe** — *when the audience holds a comfortable wrong belief.* Flip what they think the problem is.
   - "Your funnel isn't broken. Your message is." · "It was never a traffic problem. It was a clarity problem." · "You don't need more leads. You need a reason for the ones you have."
2. **The Contrarian Claim** — *when there's a loud consensus to push against (drives reposts + reply debate).* State the unpopular thing flat, no hedge.
   - "Most bad marketing isn't bad. It's invisible." · "Posting daily is not a strategy. It's a treadmill." · "Your brand voice is not your logo's job."
3. **The Blunt Imperative** — *when you want a habit dead on the spot.* Open with a command.
   - "Stop describing your service." · "Delete your second headline. Nobody read it." · "Read your homepage out loud. Then watch yourself flinch."
4. **The Diagnosis** — *as a thread opener for the messaging sequence.* Name the disease before the cure, with a promise the thread pays off.
   - "Your product isn't the problem. Your sequence is. Here's the order most founders get backwards." · "Slow sales are a symptom. Here's the message problem underneath."
5. **The Receipt / Proof** — *when you have a real, stat-bank number.* Open on the outcome, third person.
   - "A client changed one button. 1.2% to 6.5%." · "Same market. Same budget. 3 crews to 9. The message moved, not the spend."
6. **The Question (reply-bait)** — *when you want a thread of confessions to reply into (the ~150x author-reply play).* Low-stakes, specific, about their own work.
   - "What's the last line of copy that actually stopped your scroll." · "Founders. What's the one metric you'd keep if you lost the rest."
7. **The Identity Callout** — *when a specific reader should feel seen instantly.* Name who it's for in the first three words.
   - "Founders who hate marketing. This one's for you." · "If your launch flopped quietly, read this." · "Marketers drowning in dashboards. Here's the one number that matters."
8. **The Number Promise** — *as a thread/list opener; the count sets a completion loop.* Promise an exact, un-rounded count.
   - "Eight things quietly killing your conversion. Most founders are guilty of three." · "Three lines of copy worth more than your whole ad budget."
9. **The Confession** — *when you want trust before the lesson (the scars register).* Admit the bruise.
   - "I got messaging wrong for years. I taught the logo first. It cost clients money." · "Thirteen years in, the hardest system to debug is still people."
10. **The Stat** — *when one real number reframes the whole topic.* Open on the figure, then make it theirs.
    - "First impressions form in about 50 milliseconds. Your headline doesn't get a second draft in their head." · "Most of a category isn't buying this quarter. You're marketing to the 5 percent and ignoring the 95."
11. **The News React** — *in the daily-reactive pass while a story is hot (non-follower reach).* Name the thing, then turn it into a principle.
    - "Everyone's panicking about the new Google update. Panic isn't a strategy. Here's what actually changes." · "Another brand rebranded this week. New paint. Same broken message underneath."
12. **The Bold Promise** — *when the payoff is genuinely usable today; don't write a check the post can't cash.* State the value plainly, deliver it next line.
    - "The clearest message in your category isn't the cleverest. It's the one that names the wound. Here's how to find it." · "Best marketing advice you'll read today. Sell the result, not the mechanism."

## 3. Tier index (for the dealer)

- **A (lead ~70% of draws):** Two-Beat Diagnosis · Stop/Start · The Receipt · Reply-Bait Question · Contrarian One-Liner · Diagnosis Thread
- **B (solid, situational):** Steal-This Tip · Listicle Thread
- **C (experimental, ~30% quota):** The Teardown

*Why:* the A cards are X's highest-ER shapes (singles ~3.56%, threads ~3x) and are Jake's signature sentence forms — near-zero effort, fully on-voice, the ~150x author-reply lever lives in the Reply-Bait Question. B cards are only as strong as the tactic is concrete (Steal-This) or risk reading content-y (Listicle). The Teardown is reach-rich via non-follower distribution but reactive and carries reputational risk — run it through the daily-reactive pass with the "diagnose the choice, not the human" guardrail.
