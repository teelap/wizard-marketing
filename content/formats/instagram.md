## 1. Format cards

### The Send-This Carousel — Tier A
- **What:** a diagnosis-first carousel whose final slide is engineered as a "send this to a colleague" share prompt.
- **Why it works here:** sends-per-reach (DM shares) is IG's heaviest-weighted signal (per instagram.md; ~3–5x likes). A named share-prompt close ("send this to the person who handles X") turns saves into sends, and sends are the dominant driver of non-follower reach.
- **Skeleton:** slide 1 = the symptom, keyworded · 2 = the real problem (reframe) · 3–7 = the fix, one beat per slide, open loop at the foot of each ("next: where it actually breaks") · 8 = "know a marketer shipping this mistake. send them this." + book CTA. Keyword front-loaded in caption line 1; share prompt on the last slide AND restated as caption line 2.
- **Hooks that fit:** the callout, the reframe, number cold-open.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** slide 1: "the reason good products lose to worse ones." caption first line: "your sequence is broken, not your product. send this to whoever owns the messaging."
- **Don't:** a generic "follow for more" close. Ask for the SEND, name who to send it to.
- **Best for:** Myth, Pitfall, Takeaway.

### The Swipe-to-Fix — Tier A
- **What:** a tension carousel that withholds the fix until the last slide, so the swipe-to-completion is the whole point.
- **Why it works here:** completion (reaching the final slide) directly feeds distribution, and unswiped slides get resurfaced later as "new content" (instagram.md carousel mechanic) — a second free distribution pass. Withholding the fix is what earns the full swipe.
- **Skeleton:** slide 1 = the symptom as an open loop ("this is why your best line dies in paragraph three") · 2 = why the obvious fix fails · 3–6 = ruling out the wrong answers, each ending "still not it" · 7 = the actual fix, named · 8 = one-line recap + CTA. Keyword in slide 1 and caption line 1; no payoff before the last slide.
- **Hooks that fit:** the open loop, the contrarian claim, the question.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** slide 1: "you don't have a traffic problem. swipe." caption first line: "more visitors won't fix a page that doesn't convert. here's what does."
- **Don't:** front-load the answer on slide 2. If they can stop early, they will, and the resurfacing mechanic never fires.
- **Best for:** FAQ, Pitfall, Method.

### The Count Deck — Tier A
- **What:** a numbered list carousel, one item per slide, scoped by a specific number in the hook.
- **Why it works here:** bounded numbers signal credibility and a finite read (low friction = high completion), and list decks are the most reliably saved format — saves are weighted ~3x likes (instagram.md). High save rate plus a clean swipe path is the carousel's bread and butter.
- **Skeleton:** slide 1 = "[number] [things] that [outcome]," keyworded · 2 = the stakes in one line · 3 through (2+N) = one item per slide, parallel phrasing · final = "save this before your next launch" + CTA. Keyword in the slide-1 line; save prompt on the close, send prompt optional in caption line 2.
- **Hooks that fit:** number cold-open, the callout, the promise.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** slide 1: "7 lines that quietly kill a sales page." caption first line: "most bad copy isn't bad. it's invisible. here are the 7 leaks."
- **Don't:** pad to hit the number. Five real leaks beat seven with filler. Choice overload kills the save.
- **Best for:** Tip, Pitfall, Method.

### The Myth-Bust Deck — Tier A
- **What:** a two-column teardown — the believed thing, then the true thing — resolved across the swipe.
- **Why it works here:** a stated belief on slide 1 creates the knowledge gap that makes swiping the only way to close it, and "you're wrong about X" is high-comment bait — conversation depth is a ranked signal. Sits dead-center in Jake's truth-teller positioning.
- **Skeleton:** slide 1 = the myth, stated flat and keyworded ("a new logo fixes slow sales") · 2 = "here's what's actually happening" · 3–6 = the mechanism, one beat per slide · 7 = the reframe as a rule · 8 = "send this to whoever's about to rebrand" + CTA. Keyword in the myth line; send prompt names the person about to make the mistake.
- **Hooks that fit:** the contrarian claim, the callout, the reframe.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** slide 1: "a rebrand will not fix slow sales." caption first line: "the logo was never the problem. your messaging sequence is."
- **Don't:** strawman a myth nobody holds. Bust a belief your audience actually pays for.
- **Best for:** Myth, Commentary, Takeaway.

### The 3-Second Reel — Tier A
- **What:** a talking-head or text-on-screen Reel that lands the hook inside 3 seconds and pays it off in 3–5 beats.
- **Why it works here:** Reels are IG's discovery engine (most non-follower views, per instagram.md), watch time is the top-weighted signal, and skip rate punishes any Reel that doesn't earn the first 3 seconds. Front-loaded hook + muted-readable overlay is what survives the drop-off.
- **Skeleton:** beat 1 (0–3s) = hook spoken AND on-screen as a <7-word overlay, pattern interrupt in frame · beat 2 = the real problem · beats 3–4 = the fix in sequence, a cut every 1.5–3s · beat 5 = one-line takeaway + spoken CTA. Keyword in the on-screen hook + caption line 1; share prompt in caption line 2. No watermark (suppressed).
- **Hooks that fit:** the contrarian claim, the callout, number cold-open.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** beat 1 overlay: "stop describing your service." caption first line: "nobody buys the service. they buy the wound it closes. here's the rewrite."
- **Don't:** open with a slow intro or a logo sting. The hook is the first frame, not the third.
- **Best for:** Tip, Myth, Commentary.

### The Hybrid Motion Deck — Tier B
- **What:** a stills carousel with one 3–5s silent clip dropped in as a slide to spike dwell.
- **Why it works here:** instagram.md flags hybrid carousels as the emerging format — a short clip raises dwell (a top signal), and adding audio makes the whole carousel eligible for the Reels feed, opening a non-follower surface a pure stills deck never reaches.
- **Skeleton:** slide 1 = static hook, keyworded · 2 = the 3–5s clip (a desk-scene beat, a before/after, a hand sketching the sequence), readable muted, on-screen text overlay carries the line · 3–7 = static beats · final = share/save CTA. Add a track so it enters the Reels feed. Keyword in slide 1 + caption line 1.
- **Hooks that fit:** the open loop, the reframe, the question.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** slide 1: "watch where attention actually goes on your homepage." caption first line: "50 milliseconds. that's your whole first impression. here's where it leaks."
- **Don't:** bury the clip on a back slide or let it run silent with no overlay. Half the feed watches muted — the text has to carry it.
- **Best for:** Story, Method, Commentary.

### The Receipt Reel — Tier B
- **What:** a story-driven Reel built on one of Jake's documented client results, told as a mini-arc.
- **Why it works here:** narrative tension holds watch time (the top signal), and a concrete before/after number is the most re-shareable proof — sends drive the non-follower reach Reels exist for. Composite framing is allowed (CONTENT_MATRIX); the numbers stay locked to the stat bank.
- **Skeleton:** beat 1 (0–3s) = the result stated cold, overlay ("one button. one number moved.") · beat 2 = the before state · beat 3 = the one change · beat 4 = the after, with the stat-bank number · beat 5 = the rule it proves + CTA. Keyword in the hook; frame as "a client," never "I."
- **Hooks that fit:** the receipt, the open loop, the contrarian claim.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** beat 1 overlay: "one button change. 1.2 to 6.5 percent." caption first line: "a client's conversion didn't need more traffic. it needed one surgical fix."
- **Don't:** invent or round the number, or slip into first person. It's a client's result, told as his numbers. Never audited.
- **Best for:** Story, FAQ, Takeaway.

### The Quiet Card — Tier C
- **What:** a single still or 2-slide micro-carousel carrying one Jake aphorism, no explainer.
- **Why it works here:** restraint reads as confidence (the Vassallo device, VOICE_DOSSIER §4), and a flat-stated truth is high-save, high-screenshot-share — both ranked signals. It's the format that says he doesn't need the swipe. Experimental because static is IG's lowest-reach format (instagram.md) — it earns its slot on saves, not reach.
- **Skeleton:** slide 1 = the aphorism, verbatim from CONTENT_GUIDE §7, set on parchment · optional slide 2 = the one-line gloss + CTA. Keyword worked into caption line 1 (the image text is the aphorism, not the keyword).
- **Hooks that fit:** the aphorism, the reframe.
- **Example (Jake's voice):** slide 1: "content that doesn't convert is noise." caption first line: "every post should move one number. if it can't, cut it. save this where you'll see it."
- **Don't:** explain the aphorism to death or stack three. One line. Let it sit.
- **Best for:** Takeaway, Commentary.

## 2. Hook bank

Each hook is a slide-1 line and a caption opener. Rotate the attack — never open three posts the same way. All keep the keyword in caption line 1.

1. **The Callout** — *when the audience is making a specific, common mistake right now.* Loss aversion.
   - slide 1: "you're burying your best line in paragraph three." caption: "the hook that earns the first three seconds can't be your fourth sentence." · slide 1: "your homepage describes. it never diagnoses." caption: "describing your service is why nobody reads past the fold."
2. **The Reframe** — *when the audience blames the wrong cause.* The diagnosis-first two-beat, Jake's signature.
   - slide 1: "your product isn't the problem. your sequence is." caption: "good products lose to worse ones every day. here's the reason." · slide 1: "it's not a traffic problem. it's a conversion problem." caption: "more visitors won't save a page that doesn't earn the click."
3. **Number Cold-Open** — *when the post is a bounded list or a stat carries the idea.* Signals a finite, credible read.
   - slide 1: "7 lines that quietly kill a sales page." caption: "most bad copy isn't bad. it's invisible. here are the seven leaks." · slide 1: "50 milliseconds. that's your whole first impression." caption: "people judge your page before they read a word of it."
4. **The Contrarian Claim** — *when you can challenge a belief the audience pays to act on.* High-comment bait.
   - slide 1: "a rebrand will not fix slow sales." caption: "the logo was never the problem. the messaging sequence is." · slide 1: "more content is not your growth lever." caption: "you don't need to post more. you need one line that lands."
5. **The Open Loop** — *when the value is the fix and you want the full swipe / watch.* Pure curiosity gap.
   - slide 1: "you don't have a traffic problem. swipe." caption: "the real leak is somewhere you haven't looked. here it is." · slide 1: "the metric most owners never check. it's not revenue." caption: "share of brand voice. and almost nobody measures it."
6. **The Question** — *when you want the audience to self-diagnose before you answer.* Answer it immediately, never leave it hanging.
   - slide 1: "do you need more traffic, or better conversion." caption: "usually it's the second. here's how to tell which." · slide 1: "what's the last line that actually stopped your scroll." caption: "you remember it because it diagnosed you. that's the job."
7. **The Receipt** — *when a documented client result is the proof.* Frame as "a client," §2 numbers only.
   - slide 1: "one button change. 1.2 to 6.5 percent." caption: "a client's conversion problem wasn't traffic. it was one surgical fix." · slide 1: "3 crews to 9. same market, same owner." caption: "a landscaper didn't scale on ad spend. he scaled on sequence."
8. **The Confession** — *when you're owning a mistake to earn the lesson (the pratfall/scars device).* Builds trust, never humblebrags.
   - slide 1: "i got this wrong for years." caption: "i used to lead with the offer. it cost me deals i should have closed." · slide 1: "i didn't learn this in a classroom. i learned it in the trenches." caption: "twenty years in, the hardest system to debug is still people."
9. **The Aphorism** — *when one line is the whole post.* Verbatim from CONTENT_GUIDE §7. For the Quiet Card.
   - slide 1: "people browse intellectually and buy emotionally." caption: "you're not selling the spec. you're selling the feeling under it." · slide 1: "anyone can get likes, hearts, shares, and clicks." caption: "the question is whether any of it moved a number."
10. **The Stakes** — *when inaction needs to feel expensive.* Loss framed concretely, no fake urgency.
    - slide 1: "every week this line stays vague, you lose buyers who were ready." caption: "the cost of a bad hook isn't a slow week. it's the buyer who never came back." · slide 1: "the 95 percent not buying today still remember you. or they don't." caption: "brand memory gets built before the buying window opens, or never."
11. **The News React** — *when there's a fresh marketing/AI/SEO development to riff on (Hold My Beard mode).* Time-sensitive, for the daily reactive layer.
    - slide 1: "everyone's panicking about ai search. they're asking the wrong question." caption: "seo is evolving, not dead. here's what actually changed this week." · slide 1: "the new algorithm update is off its rocker. here's the only move." caption: "volatility is not a strategy problem. it's a patience problem."
12. **The Blunt Imperative** — *when the takeaway is a single action they can ship today.* For Tips.
    - slide 1: "stop describing. start diagnosing." caption: "rewrite your headline as the wound, not the service. ship it today." · slide 1: "cut your first sentence. it's a throat-clear." caption: "your real opening line is usually the second one. delete the first."

## 3. Tier index (for the dealer)

- **A (lead ~70% of draws):** Send-This Carousel · Swipe-to-Fix · Count Deck · Myth-Bust Deck · 3-Second Reel
- **B (solid, situational):** Hybrid Motion Deck · Receipt Reel
- **C (experimental, ~30% quota):** Quiet Card

*Why:* the four carousels are IG's highest-engagement format (~10%) and Jake's structural home, each engineered around the two heaviest signals — sends (Send-This, Myth-Bust name who to send to) and completion/saves (Swipe-to-Fix withholds the payoff, Count Deck is built to save). The 3-Second Reel is A because Reels are the only real non-follower discovery surface and the front-loaded hook is Jake's two-beat voice. The Hybrid Deck is emerging (needs a clip); the Receipt Reel is rate-limited by the stat bank (can't carry the rotation without repeating). The Quiet Card runs against IG's mechanics on purpose — wins on saves/screenshots, not reach; the confidence move, used sparingly. News-React and Confession hooks skew reactive — pair with the daily layer.

**Healthy spread per week:** lead with carousels (A), put one Reel in the discovery engine (A), test one B when footage or a fresh result exists, spend the C quota on a Quiet Card or a reactive News React.
