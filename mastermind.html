ME YET.

There's no application any more and there's no gate on the front door, so I'll say the quiet part here instead: $500 a month is wrong for a lot of people, and I'd rather tell you now than take a month's money to find out together.

It's worth it for a founder or marketing leader who has crossed the starting line — real product, real customers, real problems — and who will actually show up on a Tuesday and put their numbers on the screen. The calls are the product. Skip them and you're paying for a course library.

If you're pre-revenue, still deciding what you sell, or you know you won't make the calls: join free and stay free. Read the room, use the Book Readers space, come back when the calls would pay for themselves. The free side isn't a trial that runs out.

Margin note from the Wizard: the free tier isn't a loss leader I'm tolerating. A room where only paying members can speak turns into a room where nobody says anything that might look stupid — and the stupid-looking question is usually the one worth answering. The free side keeps the traffic honest.

Before You Ask

What does it cost?

Joining is free and stays free. Membership is $500 a month, or $5,000 a year if you'd rather pay once and get two months free. Either way it starts with seven days free and nothing due today, and you can cancel any time from inside the room.

What's the difference between free and paid?

Free gets you the feed, Start Here, Ask the Room and Book Readers — the parts where people talk to each other. Membership adds the parts where I'm in it: group consulting twice a week, all six course tracks, the members' lounge, and my WhatsApp. You can see the locked spaces from the free side, so you know exactly what you're deciding about.

When are the calls, and what happens on them?

Tuesdays and Thursdays at 11am Arizona — and Arizona doesn't do daylight saving, so check what that is where you are twice a year. Hot seats and case reviews. Somebody puts a real problem on the screen and we work it. Recordings go up for the members who couldn't make it, but the value is being in the seat.

Do I need to read the book first?

No. Eight Dominoes is the framework the room runs on, and there's a space for people who arrive from it — but the Eight Dominoes course track covers the same ground from domino one. The book is the cheapest way to find out whether you like how I think.

How many people are in there?

Not many yet, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. The room reopened on Circle in September 2026 and I moved everything across — all six course tracks, all 96 lessons, the calls on the calendar. What I don't have yet is a wall of member testimonials, because the people who'd write them only just walked in. If a big number is what would convince you, wait six months and check back. If getting me on a call twice a week before the room fills up sounds better than being member 4,000, come now.

Isn't this the thing that used to be called Business Arcanum?

It is. I retired that name in September 2026 and folded it into Eight Dominoes. Three brands competing for the same attention was two too many, and "Arcanum" told you nothing about what you were buying. Same room, same person running it, a name that says what it does. Anyone who joined under the old name keeps their access.
