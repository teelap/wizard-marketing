# Voice and copy

Jake Tlapek, "The Wizard of Marketing". Thirteen years in the trenches helping founders and marketers fix messaging that sells the product instead of the future the buyer wants. People don't buy features. They buy dreams. The wizard look is decoration; the substance is anti-magic: there is no magic, only psychology and work.

## Every line

- Write so a stranger with zero context understands it. Plain point first, one concrete detail, then the call to action.
- Short sentences, under 12 words on average. Two beats: a short claim, then a shorter twist ("People don't buy features. They buy dreams.").
- Second person, plain-spoken, mild deadpan. Teacher energy.
- Numbers instead of adjectives, and only approved numbers (below).
- Calls to action are orders, not asks: "Book the call", "Request the audit", "Work with the Wizard", "Join free", "Get the book".

## Never

- No emoji, anywhere. No exclamation marks.
- No "Learn more", "Submit", "Get started", "Click here".
- No corporate jargon (synergy, leverage, best-in-class, unlock, game-changer), no guru promises ("10x overnight", "secret hack"), no fake urgency, no hedging ("might", "could potentially").
- No "Get the truth". No "Business Arcanum" (retired). No "sequence" as the message.
- Never say Jake is from Chicago. Never say he hosts a podcast; he is a guest. Finch is in the past ("was Chief Evangelist at Finch").
- No scarcity or application language for the Mastermind: there is no application and no cap.
- Never draw, generate or cartoon Jake. Real photos only.
